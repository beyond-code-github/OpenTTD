# Junction tool: planar/ (the planar line/route model)

- The planar model stands beside the old pipeline and shares none of its graph, order, structure, programs or placement concepts.

## Architecture: SSS, then lengths, heights and signals (D361, D362, D365)
- The planar solver is a stack of layers. Each layer makes only its own decisions. Its output meets every constraint it owns by construction, and it hands the next layer a stated promise. No later layer repairs, retries or discovers infeasibility in an earlier layer's decisions. New work follows this stack; a generate-and-check mechanism is a defect to design out.
- **Structure** (topology, channels, sites, track map):
  - Decides what tracks exist and their planar order: the separator and the kind (bridge or tunnel) of each structure, per joiner at a join (D364); which leg runs straight at each split and merge; triangle flips, copies, rows.
  - Variants live here and nowhere else.
  - Groups crossings into structures (the crossings sharing one bridge or tunnel), each with its separator and allowed kinds (D369).
  - Promise: a valid planar map of every physical track, with sides per stretch; for each structure, the tracks it must span, which fit its kind's limit at the most compact crossing angle.
- **Shape:**
  - Decides every track's headings and the piece each crossing sits on, without lengths; a structure's crossings sit on one axis piece of its separator, at angles within its kind's limit; it never regroups (D369).
  - It makes no sideways moves.
  - The separator runs straight on an axis across its span; a tunnel's separator dives, with one ramp tile at each end on flat ground (D365).
  - Promise: legal headings, and every structure spans its crossers within its kind's limit (bridge: the longest bridge; tunnel: max_tunnel_length; a main line's: also the D228a flow bound).
- **Slots:**
  - Decides every track's sideways position in every stretch, hence every S-bend, including which fork or merge leg stays straight and every close-up at a pinned port.
  - It is the one owner of sideways moves.
  - Frames cover channels, node groups and port-side corridors.
  - No step lies inside a deck, a tunnel or its ramp tiles.
  - Promise: positions agree around every loop a pin closes, and every step can be built.
- **Lengths:**
  - Decides every piece's length, as one solve with only lower bounds left.
  - Every upper bound or exact length (port pins, the longest bridge, equal parallelograms, S-bend sizes) is an earlier layer's promise, so stretching always succeeds.
  - Promise: a compact, clash-free layout with room for its signals.
- **Heights** (D365; built after T2, T3 and LL² are end to end checker clean):
  - Decides every tile's track level and the terraform on the fixed footprint: ramps on axis pieces only, portals, bridge clearance, cuttings.
  - Promise: always feasible with terraform allowed (D218); only hard facts (houses under track or a span, water, map edge, unpurchasable) fail, named by tile, before any solve.
  - The hover runs Heights only, on the flat seed's footprint; it picks a structure's kind only within the set Structure handed down (D366).
- **Signals:** places signals and prio joins by a walk over the built tracks. The checker is a test oracle.
- Each layer depends only on the model of the layer before it; information that spans layers flows down in the hand-offs, never rediscovered or re-litigated (D366).
- Layers are designed outside-in, in stack order: a layer's design starts only when the layer before it has a settled hand-off spec; nothing is designed, probed or built on guessed inputs (D368).
- Each layer refines the model it receives, never undoing an earlier decision, and owns its invariants: it establishes and tests them at its own output; later layers rely on them unchecked, and a broken invariant is the owning layer's bug (D367).
- Headings need no lengths: a shape is angles only, as on a metro map drawn with fixed angles, and the length layer only stretches pieces.
- Variants are ranked on cheap scores (structure, shape and slot costs) as they pass down; Lengths solves the best few and picks by its own model (D366).

## Terms
- **Hub:** a generated junction. **Port:** where track enters or leaves the hub's box, on one side, with one or more lines side by side.
- **Route:** where a train goes, from an entry port to an exit port. **Line:** one strand of track between two events; routes share a line until they split.
- **Split / merge:** one line becoming two, or two becoming one.
  - **Fray:** a split whose legs reach different ports. **Lane split:** a split whose legs reach the same port.
  - **Join:** a merge from different entries. **Lane merge:** a merge from the same entry.
  - **Fork:** a split with no straight leg.
- **Crossing:** two lines that must cross. One is the **separator**: it carries the **structure** and runs straight across its span, on a bridge (its **deck**) or in a tunnel. The structure's **kind** is bridge or tunnel. **Over/under** is the height that follows: a bridge's separator is over, a tunnel's under.
- **Topology:** ports, lines, splits, merges, crossings and their order along each line. No positions.
- **Channel:** lines that travel together, with the same start, the same destination and the same crossings.
- **Site:** where channels split or merge. Its **straight** channel continues straight; the other leaves or arrives on a **side**.
- **Tie:** a site, or a split or merge, whose straight leg the rules cannot pick; the choice is a variant bit.
- **Track map:** the topology with every physical track added:
  - **copies:** a full-share lane doubled over a bridge (D351);
  - **prio rows:** the track beside a lane at a prio join (D349).
- **Variant:** one combination of the structure's choices (over bits, ties, triangle flips), tagged `o..t..f..s..`.
- **Heading:** one of 8 directions; a track turns at most 45 degrees at a time. **Piece:** a straight run at one heading.
- **Shape:** per track, its heading list and the piece each crossing sits on.
- **Slot:** a track's sideways position in its bundle, in lane spacings. **S-bend:** the sideways move a slot change makes (turn 45 degrees, run diagonally, turn back). **Frame:** tracks whose slots share one zero.
- **Lengths / the length program:** the integer program choosing every piece's length for the least area.

## Topology (`planar_topology`)
- A topology is text in the engineer's notation: `port <side> <in|out> <heading>: lines` (lines left to right across travel; a side's ports in reading order, N and S west to east, W and E north to south), `node in -> out`, `prio routes`.
- A line name is capital letters, a lane number and an optional lower-case suffix; `CB1` carries routes C1 and B1; `name=R1+R2` names its routes explicitly.
- A node statement is a group of 1 -> 2 splits (fewer in than out) or 2 -> 1 merges (more in than out); a split's legs are the out lines whose routes lie in its in line's, in authored order; a merge's legs are the in lines whose routes lie in its out line's.
- A route is a lane path; its paths are every walk over lines carrying it from its one entry port line to an exit port line; a choice route has several.
- The ring is the port lines clockwise from the north-west corner: each port's lines enter in reading order when right of travel points along reading order, else reversed.
- Two paths of different routes that share no line must cross iff their ends interleave on the ring; paths sharing a line never cross.
- A crossing is a pair of lines; it crosses every path through one with every path through the other, so a line pair is admissible only when all those pairs must cross. Port lines (a port at either end) never cross.
- Crossings are chosen greedily: admissible pairs covering the most path pairs first, then by line index, each path pair covered once; a must-cross pair left uncovered refuses the topology.
- Event order: each line's crossings in the order a straight chord of its first path meets the other lines' chords, ports on a circle at uneven angles and each port's lines packed together, so bundles cross whole and the orders agree.
- The T is authored by `PlanarTText(lanes)`: the engineer's drawing turned half a turn into the canonical frame (main W-E, branch S): A E->S, B E->W, C S->W, D S->E, E W->E, F W->S; B and E hold prio.
- At one lane the T has exactly three crossings (B×D, B×F, D×F); at two lanes 18: the 12 core crossings on single-route lines and one fray crossing at each of the six rings.

## Channels (`planar_channels`)
- `BuildPlanarChannels` groups a topology's lines into channels: lines with the same in ports reaching them, the same out ports reached, the same depth from the in ports and the same crossed channels in order (refined until stable); a line may end inside its channel at a node of its own while the others carry on.
- A channel's lines are ordered left to right across travel: port lines by the port's order, other lines by their leftmost in leg's order, then their leg position.
- A crossing whose two lines start or end at the same node group belongs to that group (a fray's or join's own crossings); every other crossing belongs to the pair of channels, and a channel's crossings follow its first line's event order.
- The T at one, two and three lanes gives twelve channels (EF, AB, CD, ED, CB, AF and the six movements A to F) and three whole channel crossings (B x D, B x F, D x F); in LL², E1 and E2 share a channel and the crossing choice legs are channels of their own.

- A site is the splits leaving one channel or the merges entering one channel (grouped by channel, not by node statement): a fray when its legs reach different out ports, a lane split when the same; a join when its legs come from different in ports, a lane merge when the same.
- A site's straight channel is the one carrying a prio route when exactly one does, else the one turning least between its port headings among the prio channels (among all, with none); a tie leaves it open as a variant (LL²'s entry frays: E straight, F leaving right). The side is the other channel's turn direction (+1 right). On the T this gives E and B straight with F leaving right and A left, D joining from the right and C from the left, and ties at the S fray and S join (the old `fray_s` and `join_s` variants).
- Per node (`PlanarChannels::node_legs`): a split's straight leg is the one in its site's straight channel, with the site's side; a split whose legs go to one port is a fork (no straight leg). A merge's straight leg is its line holding prio (a prio route runs on it), else the leg turning least; the other leg's side is its turn direction, or its place in the node's list (left to right across travel) when it does not turn. LL²: E1 runs straight at EO1 with EDj1 joining from the right; E2a straight at EDj1 with D1a from the right; E2 forks.
## Channel paths (`planar_paths`)
- At a channel crossing a prio channel is over a non-prio one (the main line stays straight); between two prio or two non-prio channels the over channel is a choice (`PlanarOpenCrossings`, one bit each in `BuildPlanarPaths`).
- A channel turns the short way from its start heading to its single out port's heading, 45 degrees a piece; its start heading is its in port's when it starts at a port, else the heading its in channel ends on (falling back to the one it has); its crossings sit in its event order on the first piece at or after the last that suits (axis when over, not parallel to the over piece when under), with a back-and-forth detour only when none suits (a straight fork leg instead jogs toward its own side: the left leg left, the right leg right); the pieces settle jointly: a piece suits given the other channel's current piece (before that is chosen, only a piece one of its pieces or a fork leg's jog could cross, unless none could), passes repeat until no piece changes, and channels are visited over first where the overs have no cycle (a cycle of overs is composed).
- An empty crossing triangle (three channels crossing pairwise, each with its two crossings adjacent) can sit either way round; `PlanarTriangles` finds them and `FlipPlanarTriangles` swaps each flipped triangle's crossings on its three channels; the mapped solve tries both (a variant bit each). The T has one (B, D, F); the hand compositions use the flipped one.
- The shape of a variant is its channel paths (per channel the piece headings and the piece each crossing sits on); `BuildPlanarPaths` takes one as an optional argument instead of deciding it, and returns it as given with `why` set by `CheckPlanarShape`. `CheckPlanarShape` holds the rules a shape must obey: one path per channel; headings 45 degrees apart; the first heading the up port's (or the in channel's last), the last the down port's, not opposite; one crossing piece per crossing, in range and in travel order; at each crossing the over piece on an axis and the under piece not parallel to it. The default shape is `BuildPlanarPaths`' own: `PlanarDefaultShape(topology, spec, tag)` gives it for a variant tag, and `SolvePlanarMappedVariant(topology, spec, tag, shape, layout)` solves exactly that variant on a given shape (null: the default); a variant solved on its own default shape gives the layout it gives without one.
- On the T: B is over D and F, D x F is the one open crossing; B and E are straight, F E-SE-S, D N-NE-E, C N-NW-W, A W-SW-S; the topology's event order puts D's B crossing before its F crossing (F x D between the main lines), unlike hand composition (F x D south of B).

## Layout program (`planar_layout`)
- The layout is one integer program through `JunctionMilp` in half-tile units: per line a chain of vertices (start, one spare bend plus the route's least turn count per stretch, each crossing, end), per segment a heading in 8 and a length; a node is one vertex shared by its lines, a crossing one vertex shared by its two lines.
- A line's segments keep to the short turn arc of its routes plus one heading beyond each end.
- A bend vertex with no curve starts a zero-length segment unless the next vertex is an event; a zero-length segment keeps the heading before it; segments at a node, port or crossing vertex have length.
- Ports: the port line's end segment has the port heading and at least 4 units; a port's lines are adjacent, the two ports of a side 2 (gap + 1) apart (ml_gap on W and E, branch_gap on S).
- A split's left leg turns left or not at all, its right leg right or not at all, and they differ; a merge's left leg turns left into the trunk, its right leg right; each leg has at least 2 units before its next event.
- A merge where one leg carries a prio route: that leg is straight into the merge, axis, for n_prio + 1 tiles; the element's row tiles, on the side opposite the joiner, hold no other track.
- A crossing's over line is axis at C, the under line not parallel; the over line has 3 + (under diagonal) units each side and 2 more on the approach side (the axis piece `RasterizeJunctionRoute`'s B starts from) unless its deck merges from the crossing before; a prio line is never over (doubling is not built in this model).
- Consecutive over crossings of one line with no curve between share one deck within the longest middle; otherwise a curve lies between.
- A non-prio route runs at least 2 TL units from its last split or port to each merge.
- Clashes (`JunctionTracksMayShareTile`, touching across a tile edge, heads exclusive, deck middles over ground allowed), curve gaps (`JunctionCurveGaps` on the solved hands), prio element room and terrain facts are added lazily as separations or floors, each holding while the headings it was found at hold, and the program is solved again until none is found.
- `PLANAR_DEBUG` set in the environment prints the lazy loop's rounds.
- `PLANAR_IIS` set in the environment: a mapped solve proven infeasible prints a minimal conflicting row set to stderr, `IIS <tag> : <row>` per row (tags: `seg <strand>#<i>`, `cross`, `deck`, `sep`, `fan rule`, `box`, ...), or `IIS integer-only (LP feasible)`.
- `PLANAR_FACESEPS` (experiment): before the first solve, every pair of lines bounding a common face (`PlanarLineSides::FacePairs`) is separated segment against segment on the side `Side` gives, pairs sharing an end point left out; it makes every T2, T3 and LL² variant infeasible (IIS core on T2: the split node's incoming line and its two legs get opposite sides of the leg's far segment, because a side holds only where lines run alongside, not along the whole strand).
- `PLANAR_ALIGN` (experiment): a prio route between opposite sides enters and leaves on the same row; `PLANAR_ALIGN=W` only routes entering from the west. With today's steps it is infeasible on every T2/T3 variant and on all but one of 201 LL² variants (o49t0f1s0: 1738.25, 104x65), because no step brings a fanned main line back.

## Back end (`planar_plan`)
- The flat seed is cached per topology text and the spec's rule fields. It solves the component layout (`SolvePlanarComponents`) for topologies with a composition (LL² by its hand composition, every other topology but the one-lane T by `SolvePlanarMapped`; `seed.components`, `bound` 0) and the layout program (`planar_layout`) otherwise, at box bound 64 tiles, then 128; a topology with no composition behaves as before.
- A component plan renders the cached programs through `RenderPlanarRoutePrograms` (flat placement even on terrain, levels solved after as for any plan); the hover reuses them.
- `RenderPlanarRoutePrograms`: each program through `RasterizeJunctionRoute` (turn +1 is the rasteriser's L, a deck is B<middle>), tags edge = the path line and copy = the program's copy, `JunctionTrackRoute` index = the running index of (route, path), signal routes with arrivals, switches, merge nodes and the programs' copy switches sorted by piece, node tiles from the programs (a split's the first piece of its out leg, a merge's the last piece of its in leg), port tiles the first (in) or last (out) piece of a program on that line, layout size from the tracks' bounding box.
- On terrain the program runs again with terrain facts lazy, warm-started from the seed; the hover fixes the seed's binaries.
- Each route path renders to one `JunctionTrackRoute` through `RasterizeJunctionRoute` (F, L, R per piece, B<middle> per deck), tagged edge = line, copy 0; the signal pass reads a `JunctionSignalNetwork` filled natively (main = the route holds prio, group = its letter).
- `PlanarCheckerTopology` gives the checker the ports as lane groups in slot order and every route path as a lane path with its route's prio flag.
- `PlanarLL2Text` is the PSG 227 pattern at two lanes; its 18 lane paths equal the composed hub's (`ComposeJunctionHub` on the width-2 T).
- `PlanarHubText` maps the window's hub type: one lane each way to the T, two with any pairing to LL², two otherwise to the two-lane T.

## Component solver (`planar_components`)
- `PlanarComponentLayout::wasted` is the wasted sideways travel of one program per route (copy 0, path 0): per program the sum of sideways tile steps minus the net sideways move, sideways being the axis across the program's net displacement; pv prints it after the track.
- `PlanarComponentLayout::empty` is the outline area minus the distinct tiles holding any piece of any route program (every path and copy); pv prints it after wasted.
- The integer program (half-tile geometry, expressions, strands, decks, crossings, the solve) lives in `planar_model` (namespace `PlanarModel`); the composers build on it.
- `Model::AddPiece` adds a required piece: an axis piece at least a tile, a diagonal at least one piece, at most an eighth's turn from the piece before, a strand starting on a diagonal starts on a tile-edge midpoint. `Model::PieceCurves` writes every route's curve rows over required pieces as sums of the pieces between curves, with no switches; it refuses an optional segment.
- `SolvePlanarComponents` has a hand composition for `LL2` (`PlanarLL2Text()`) only; T2 goes to `SolvePlanarMapped` (D326); every route line of the composition must exist in the topology's lines; the one-lane T is refused (it keeps the layout program), and any other topology goes to `SolvePlanarMapped`.
- `MappedComposer` builds in depth order (a line's depth: in port lines 0, else one more than its deepest in line): at each depth the sites whose out lines start there, then the channels starting there (bundles, then straight cores); a core's decks over the channels it crosses are wired after every channel exists.
- `SolvePlanarMapped` composes from the channel graph alone (`MappedComposer`): n-lane fray, join, doubled core and bundles, placed by the sites (straight channel, side, prio) and paths (headings, crossing pieces, over channel). Its variants are every choice of the open crossings, the tie sites, the triangles and the candidate steps (tag `o{over}t{ties}f{flips}s{steps}`; up to 6 bits every variant is solved, one thread each; above 6 bits a local search from the all-zero variant replaces the refusal: one-bit flips, the best improving neighbour (area down by more than 0.01, or the current variant failed and the neighbour solved; ties to the lower tag), 4 solves at once (`SEARCH_THREADS`), every outcome cached by tag and solved once; `PlanarComponentLayout::variants_solved` counts the variants solved; LL² reaches o17t0f1s0 at 1347.25 in 48 solves). A straight prio channel arrives on the join-entry lines from its join's far lane. Doubling follows each line's flow share (`PlanarChannels::line_share`): an in port line carries a full lane, a fray's legs keep their in line's share, a lane split's k legs take 1/k each, a merge's out line the larger of its in lines', and a line of a movement without a prio route counts half (sideline traffic: the T's S port movements); a line with a full share is doubled over a crossing, others cross single. A straight channel over crossings with full-share lanes doubles each lane once over them all (far lane copying outward, the others away from it); a turning channel's crossing piece with two full-share lanes doubles both outward (left lane's copy left, right lane's right), and a crossing under a piece holding its own decks (a doubled turning piece, or a straight channel's run) sits on its run before or after them by the channel's crossing order; deck ends sit on whole pieces; a non-prio route has a whole-train bay (2 TL + 4) from its last split or port to each merge. Clash sides are given up front from the channel graph:
  - in-port fan order: a lane right of another keeps its fan, its run and its legs' first two segments right of and ahead of the other's stub and fan;
  - ports on one side: a joiner arriving from the side of the neighbouring in port keeps its last two segments on its own side of that port's lines.

  Optional steps share one binary per site or channel and heading (`Model::opt_group`). `PLANAR_NORULES` and `PLANAR_NOTIE` switch these off, for measurement. A piece that is both over (its own deck) and under keeps the crossing outside its deck, on the side its channel's crossing order gives (given up front; `PLANAR_NOUNDER` turns it back to lazy rounds). A prio fray's fan and every bundle piece are required pieces; a plain fray has no fan. The only optional pieces left in the mapped composer are a fork's legs. A one-lane merge's exit step is a required `Turn(h, +1)` piece or none, decided before the solve: onto a port line (`PlanarMergeExitStep`) one close-up per pinned gap, +1 exactly when the joiner arrives from the right and the out line's ring successor (no wrap) is on the same box side, so the step owner of a gap is the out line before it and it steps toward the next line; onto an inner line (`PlanarInnerMergeExitStep`) the lane closing up after a lane split's spread, +1 exactly when another merge of the site has its out line to the right in the out channel's line order, whatever the joiner side. An n-lane exit's step is a required piece present exactly when the gap steps hold a close-up for its channel (toward the right only; a left one is refused); a straight channel's step is a variant bit of its candidate (`PlanarCandidateSteps`), a required diagonal in every lane, equal in length, before its core. The objective is track length plus one linearisation of the outline area: on T3 it matches the full loop on every variant, so a variant is 2 solves. Every variable is integer (only-switches-integer gave 1960 where the exact solve gives 1941). Every coordinate carries a tiny weight (0.001, `PLANAR_PULL`) pulling ties toward the north-west: on T3 and T2 it is 20-25% faster and finds T3's 1941.25, which the run without it missed by a tile. The mapped composer also anchors the layout: the box low edges X0, Y0 are even columns (whole-tile translation) and the first port's first line's point is pinned to a window 4096 <= u <= 4097 on each axis, which removes the sliding symmetry (T2 1.8 s from 2.8, T3 3.3 s from 4.1, LL² 74 s from 92); the output shifts every coordinate by (sx, sy) computed from the solution (sx the largest even number <= the least x of any strand or prio-row point, sy the largest even <= the least y - 1), and width and height come from the shifted points. Experiments: `PLANAR_OBJ` (len, box, routes; loop for the full area loop), `PLANAR_TIMES` (solve and round timings on stderr). Width plus height adds nothing over track length on T3 (the box is already tight); the area step trims the outline's empty corners. A site that is a lane split or merge, or has other than two channels, is composed per node: a lane split is a fork (each leg an optional outward diagonal, the legs at least a line apart after it, so the solver picks which runs straight), a merge a one-lane join (its straight leg and side from the node's legs, a node without a straight leg a tie bit; the joiner may land from the next line). `PLANAR_ONLY` also lifts the 6-bit variant cap (the enumeration, not the search); `PLANAR_DRY` builds every variant's default shape without solving (at most `SEARCH_THREADS` at a time) and prints, after all have run, one line per variant in enumeration order with its over choices and its construction failure.
- Mapped T3 (full run) finds 1941.25 (o0t1f1s1, 129x45) in 0.64 s wall and passes the checker; o0t1f1s0 gives 1960.25. Crossings under a prio (doubled) channel are at right angles where a back-and-forth allows it (the hand T3's D path). Mapped T2 finds 906.25 (89x34, o0t2f1s1) in 0.85 s, where the hand composition had 935. The mapped one-lane T (325.25) breaks the bay and adjacent-split rules (a known gap, hidden test).
- Four variants per hand composition (LL²: S fray side x diagonal west fray), each built and solved on its own thread; the least area inside the outline wins, ties by variant order.
- The model translates `wip/planar-probe8.py`: strands of segments in half-tile units, optional segments (binary z, length 0 or in [lo, hi]) between pieces of one heading, decks, crossings, prio rows and doubled-lane copies; curve rows follow whole routes and lapse with the optional curves they span.
- Each solve is a fresh `JunctionMilp` with every variable integer and parity auxiliaries, warm-started from the model's last solution, with a 5 s time limit (`JunctionMilp::SetTimeLimit`) cut to the time left of its variant's deadline; a solve that reaches it counts as infeasible. The 15 s budget is per variant, one deadline shared by every solve in it (rough, ladder passes, area pass, both shapes): a rough solve past it fails the variant, an unfinished ladder pass leaves the accepted solution, an unfinished second shape leaves the first shape's result.
- Clashes are separated lazily (one pair per round, first of four sorted directions that stays feasible); the area inside the outline is then minimised by sequential linearisation around the clash-free solution.
- The output is one `PlanarRouteProgram` per route path and copy choice: pieces (tile, heading 0 N .. 7 NW, Ground/Head/Middle, line index in the path) and copy switches; routes and paths are matched to the topology by route name and the comma-joined line names.
- On the test spec the solver finds LL² at 800 tiles (variant `-1F1w1`, 64x47).

## Gap steps (`planar_gaps`)
- `ResolvePlanarSites` decides each channel-graph site as the mapped composer does (same order; a per-node site keeps the defaults; tie bits consumed in site order): straight channel, other channel, side (the turn direction between the port headings) and the straight channel's prio; sites whose channels lack a single port heading refuse.
- `BuildPlanarGapSteps` walks the ring: consecutive port lines (no wrap-around) on one side of the box with different channels are a pinned gap, looked at from each channel X with Y across it. Y lies to X's left for an in line's ring successor or an out line's predecessor, else right.
- An in channel X whose resolved fray (not per node) has its other leg's side toward Y sends a leg into the gap: with a prio fray the step is X's fan (`fan` true, neighbour Y); otherwise, when Y's line is an out line, Y's close-up steps (`fan` false, neighbour X); otherwise the gap is refused (a plain fray's leg runs toward a neighbouring in port).
- An out channel X whose resolved join has its joiner's side toward Y has a joiner landing in the gap: X's close-up steps (`fan` false, neighbour Y).
- A step's `toward` is where it moves, +1 right of the stepping channel's travel: a close-up toward its neighbour (where the neighbour lies for the stepping channel, seen from its own port line), a fan away from it.
- `PlanarCandidateSteps`: a channel with one up and one down port at equal headings and crossings is a candidate when its in port has exactly one neighbour on the same side of the box in another port (ring predecessor at +1, successor at -1, no wrap); `toward` is minus that value. On the T it is B's channel, toward the left.
- Steps are deduplicated by (channel, fan), the first kept, and sorted by channel then fan (false first). On the T2 and T3 (ties `o0t2f1` and `o0t1f1`) they are EF's fan toward CB (W), AF's close-up toward CD (S) and ED's close-up toward AB (E).

## Slot layer (`planar_slots`)
- `BuildPlanarSlots(topology, channels, sites, over)` computes the sideways slot of every track of every channel interval for one variant (channels flipped, sites resolved, `PlanarPaths::over`). The composer reads it for every lane offset, fray gap and join out gap.
- A channel is cut at its crossings in travel order into `pre`, `x<k>`, `mid<k>` and `post`; a channel with no crossing has `pre` and `post`. Each interval lists its tracks left to right: the channel's lines, a `Copy` beside each full-share line in an `x<k>` where the channel is over (the far line, the one away from a prio join's joiners or the left one without such a join, has its copy on its outer side, every other line on the side away from the far line); the copy also runs through `mid<k>` between two consecutive doubled crossings of the channel. There are no row tracks yet (`Row` is an unused kind).
- A slot is an integer in lane spacings. Rules: consecutive tracks differ by at least 1; between lines in the last interval of a prio fray's channel by at least 2; a copy is exactly 1 from its line; a port line's slot is the channel's free offset plus its rank in `pre` (in port) or `post` (out port); a straight line across a split or merge site is linked to its successor by a charged step.
- A doubled line keeps its slot from one interval to the next when the next interval holds its copy too (one parallelogram over consecutive doubled crossings). After the last doubled crossing a step back from the copy's slot is charged (one per lane spacing); staying on it is free. The move onto the copy is not charged.
- A prio fray's straight legs off a non-far lane are fixed one slot toward the far lane. A prio join's straight channel keeps the entry gaps in its last interval: e = 0, 1, 3, 5, ... from the far lane.
- One `JunctionMilp` integer program minimises the steps of every line between consecutive intervals (except onto a copy) and the site link steps (weight 1 each), with the width of every interval with two or more tracks as a tie-break only (weight 1e-4). There are no yes/no variables.
- Output `PlanarSlots`: per channel and interval the tracks with slots, each channel normalised so its least slot is 0; `links` (from line, to line, step as solved, in the solver's frame where each channel has its own free offset); `objective`. A non-optimal solve leaves `channels` empty and `why` = "no slots: <status>"; the solve has a 30 s limit.
- A line's net step is its slot change over its channel plus the step of the link into it; the gap change of two neighbouring lines is the difference of their net steps. T2 `o1t3f1s0` has objective 4.0041, LL² `o17t0f1s0` 3.0054; each builds in a few ms in Release.

- The composer reads each lane's offset from the previous lane as at least its spacing in the interval's track list (copies counted, `MinGap`), not equal to the slot difference, except the run into a join (`run offset`, equal); it takes fray gaps and join out gaps from the slots; each slot change is an S-bend (an axis piece then a 45-degree piece of length 2|delta|), and a slot step on a diagonal piece is refused ("a slot step on a diagonal piece is not composed").

## Line sides (`planar_regions`)
- `PlanarLineSides` reads from the topology alone which side of line q's travel line p lies on: the planar map (vertices the ring's port ends, nodes and crossings; edges the lines' stretches between events plus the ring's steps; rotations clockwise) is built and validated (V - E + F = 2, every face a simple walk, one outer face of ring edges), and where p and q bound a common face, p lies on the side of q where that face lies.
- The face map follows the variant's channel crossing order: the constructor reorders the line-level events of each channel whose crossing order differs from `BuildPlanarChannels(topology)` before building the map (failing with `Why` when a line's moved events are not contiguous). `Faces()` exposes every face but the outer one as runs of walls: maximal runs of one dart direction in walk order (`forward`: the face lies left of the line's travel), each stretch as (line, stretch index), ring edges skipped, `box` when a ring edge bounds the face. A flipped T2 has a face walking forward, backward, forward, backward (four runs); unflipped faces have at most two.
- `Side(p, q)` is per ordered pair, relative to q's travel, with no symmetry between `Side(p, q)` and `Side(q, p)`: +1 right, -1 left, 0 for the same line, crossing lines, lines sharing no face, and pairs two faces give opposite sides (which also sets `Why`, so the composer fails). `Why` is also set when the face map fails validation. `LineOfStrand` maps a composer strand to its line by removing a trailing copy mark.
- `MappedComposer` builds one per variant and fails with its `Why`. In the lazy loop a round separates every clash whose two strands map to different lines with a non-zero side at once, each by its best `SepOptions` direction f with `Dot(f, Right(h_q)) * Side(p, q) < 0` (q at least 2 beyond p along f, so p lies on q's -f side), then solves once; when that batch is infeasible each such clash is tried on its own over the directions its side allows, in `SepOptions` order, keeping the first that stays feasible (cumulative), and only if none is kept is it "infeasible with topology sides". A round with no such clash separates `bad[0]` by trial solves as before; the hand compositions pass no sides and always do. `PLANAR_TIMES` prints each round's decided count.
- The mapped solve re-solves each variant with corridor rungs from its rough solve (`LadderRungs`, at most two passes, the second only when a rung point left its facing span; the lazy clash loop still runs in every solve and `PLANAR_NOLADDER=1` turns the ladders off). A rung holds an end point of a face's wall run at least `RungSpacing(target, own)` (1 or 2) from the facing segment of another run, or of a non-neighbouring stretch of its own run.
- Each ladder pass starts HiGHS from the accepted solution (rough, or the previous pass): the model's `last` is set from it when the column counts match.
- Rules: every face run's end points against the facing segments of the other runs and of non-neighbouring stretches of its own run, points shared with the other wall and points off the wall (stretch ends that continue past an event) dropped; the spacing is 2 for a diagonal target or an axis target on the same axis as the own segment, else 1; bridge copies join the walls on the face side as wall segments, their deck middles exempt against ground.
- `PlanarLineSides::Events()` is each line's events in the variant's channel order, which the stretch indices of `Faces()` count; the composer maps stretch k to the segments from event k - 1 to event k, with `ladder_faces` and `ladder_off` filled at the end of `Build`.
- Row tags (`TagScope`) are per thread: variants build and solve on parallel threads.

## Shape program (`planar_shape`)
- `SolvePlanarShape` finds the least-turning channel shape obeying the crossing rules, per variant (over choice and flips); nothing in the solve calls it yet. `PlanarShapeTurns` measures the same objective on any shape.
- The side rule: at each channel crossing the under line crosses from the side the topology gives (`PlanarLineSides::CrossingFrom(crossing, line)`: +1 arriving from the left of the other line's travel, -1 from its right, read from the face left of the other line's stretch ending there), so `H_under - H_over = s * a + 8q`, a in 1..3, s = CrossingFrom of the under line (every line crossing of a channel pair gives the same s). `CheckPlanarShape` refuses a shape crossing from the other side (reason names "side"); the default shapes always satisfy it. `SolvePlanarShape` takes the `PlanarLineSides` of the variant's channels.
- `SolveMappedVariant` with no shape given solves each variant on the default shape and on `SolvePlanarShape`'s shape (same channels and over) when that differs, and keeps the smaller area (a failed solve never wins; a tie goes to the default); `PlanarComponentLayout::program_shape` says which won. `PLANAR_SHAPE=default` forces the default shape only; `PLANAR_SHAPE=program` forces the program's shape, falling back to the default when the program fails (stderr line under `PLANAR_TIMES`).

## Track map (`planar_regions`)
- `BuildPlanarTrackMap(topology, channels, sites, over, joiner_over)` expands a variant's skeleton (the topology with its events in the variant's channel order) into every physical track as one planar map (DESIGN-tracks §16), validated by the same `PlanarMap` as `PlanarLineSides` (V - E + F = 2, simple faces). `PlanarTrackMap` holds the tracks (lines first at their topology index, then the parts lines are split into, then copies and rows), `part_of`, `expanded` (the expanded topology, tracks as its lines with ends and nodes), `events`, `crossings`, `faces`, and `side` per stretch pair.
- One expansion op, a parallel track beside a track over a range of its events, on a side: the track is split at a new split node before the range and at a merge node after it (a row runs to the track's end node instead); the new track mirrors each crossing in the range, inserted in the crosser's events before the original when the crosser arrives from the new track's side, else after. The arrival side of each crossing comes from `PlanarLineSides::CrossingFrom`; a mirrored crossing keeps its originals' arrival sides. Parts and the new track take the track's ring ends.
- Expanded, in this order: a row (`prio:<line>`, side away from the joiners) beside each lane of a prio join, from its first crossing with a joiner (or after its last event) to its end node; inside a prio join, with `joiner_over` false each lane S[j] (j >= 1) gets a copy on the joiners' side over its crossings with J[0..j-1], with it true each full-share joiner gets a copy on the joiners' side over its crossings with the lanes after its own (no joiner is full share on T2, T3 or LL², so it adds no copy there); a copy beside each full-share line over each run of consecutive crossings where its channel is over, on the slot layer's copy side.
- A side holds per stretch pair (p, ps, q, qs): where two stretches bound a common face, p lies on the side of q that face lies on. A pair two faces give opposite sides is a conflict unless the tracks, or the topology lines they belong to, cross. T2, T3 and every LL² variant with paths build with no conflict in both forms; at T2 o1t3f1s0 with `joiner_over` the E join's order after the crossing is J0, row, S1, J1.
- `BuildPlanarLineHeadings(topology, channels, paths, sites, events, map)` (`planar_paths`) gives every line, sites included, its heading list (DESIGN-tracks §16.7) and the piece each event sits on, plus the parent range (`beside`) of each copy and row; `why` names the first rule that failed. (1) A line's base list is its channel's path with its channel crossings on the path's `crossing_piece`. (2) A leg leaving a split node that is not straight keeps the in line's last heading turned by its side as its first piece (the base list's first piece dropped when its second equals that turn and no event sits on it), a fork's left leg taking side -1 and the right +1; the straight leg keeps its base list; the straight leg and side come from the site's resolved straight channel and side, or from the node's own legs for a site composed per node. (3) A leg entering a merge node mirrors this at its end with the turn of the out line's first heading by minus its side. (4) A crossing inside a site's group has the straight channel's line over (the node's straight leg for a per-node site) and sits on the first piece of each line that suits counting from the start at a split group, the last counting back from the end at a merge group; the over piece is axis, the under piece not parallel to it.
