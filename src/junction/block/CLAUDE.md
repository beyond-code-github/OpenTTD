# Junction tool: block/ (block programs and the block generator)

- A block program is data (`JunctionBlockProgram`): named points, lines, nodes, edges as segment lists (Run, SBend, Bridge), lane paths and prios. The generic generator `BuildJunctionBlock` turns any program into columns, one route per path, a layout and ports; a new block is a new table, not new code. `JunctionJoinBlock227Program` is the PSG 227 join block (two bundles in, two lanes out) and `JunctionJoinBlock227Topology` its topology. `JunctionExitBlockProgram` is the exit block (one input arm of two lanes, two bundles out, one doubled crossing) and `JunctionExitBlockTopology` its topology.
- The block heads east on Trackdir X_SW. The routes, layout, ports and prio spans share one frame: x is the column, y the program line, so line 0 is row 0 and the prio rows (lines 0 and 7 of the 227 block) are the first and last rows. In-port tiles are at column 0 and out-port tiles at column E. The layout size is the tight bounding box, (E + 1) x (largest line + 1), with no margin: the checker's boundary is the port sides (D300), so the prio rows may lie on the bottom edge.
- `JunctionJoinBlock227Topology` lists its prio movements: fA to L0 and fB to L1 (D298); the choice paths are plain merges and their bridges may be single (D299).
- A point is a column variable. Columns are the least solution (`JunctionPositions`) of difference floors, with W fixed at 0.
- A point is the column of the first tile of the feature that starts there: a split node is its split tile (the trunk's axis piece and the diverging leg's first half piece), a merge node its merge tile (the through axis piece and the joining leg's last half piece), a bridge point its head tile, the point a Run goes to the column of the next segment's first tile, E the out-port tile. An edge that ends in an S-bend at a node ties the node's point to the column of the bend's last half piece.
- An S-bend of k lines is `JunctionBendMoves(hand, 2|k|, false)`, with the hand whose raster closes k lines over (+ is south). Its width, its pieces by column and line, its piece units and its bends' unit positions are measured from that raster, never typed. A bend starts on an axis piece, which belongs to the run before it.
- Along a path the unit position of any feature is 2 * column + c, where c is a constant built from measured shapes: an S-bend adds its units minus 2 * its width. So every unit floor is a difference floor between two points.
- Rule families (DESIGN-blocks §3), each floor carries its family as its reason:
  - R1: the first node or bend of an in edge is at least 2 columns from W, and E is at least 2 columns from the last node or bend end of an out edge.
  - R2, R3: a plain axis tile between the S-bend before a bridge and its near head, and between its far head and the next S-bend.
  - R4: the crossed edge's S-bend pieces on the bridge's line lie strictly between the heads, each head `JunctionUnrelatedTileGap` from the nearest one.
  - R5: on a non-through path, the units from the piece after its last upstream split to the last piece before a merge whose tile holds no other edge are at least 2 TL.
  - R6: a plain axis tile between a merge tile, or a split tile on its straight leg, and the next S-bend.
  - R7: on each line, consecutive occupants of unrelated edges keep `JunctionUnrelatedTileGap`, or share a tile where `JunctionTracksMayShareTile` allows. Related is one edge, or two edges that meet at a node, at that node's tile. The occupants are runs, S-bend pieces, bridge heads and the prio rows, never a bridge's middle or the S-bend pieces a bridge passes over.
  - R8: `JunctionCurveGaps` on every path's bends; a gap inside one S-bend must hold already, any other is a floor between points.
  - R9: a prio's merge is at least P (the spec's `prio_tiles` rounded up to even) plus 2 columns from W, and its fixed track has no node and no bend within P columns.
- R5 and R7 need the pieces: pass 1 solves every other family, the rasters of pass 1 give R5's end piece and R7's occupant order, and pass 2 solves with all floors. Pass 2 must keep every line's occupant order and leave no unrelated pair touching, or the build fails.
- The block's pieces are checked against the program's own item list, so a measure error fails the build.
- The prio rows (lines 0 and 7 of the 227 block) are occupants only: no route is drawn on them; the signal pass draws the prio spans there.
- Errors are strings; no data error asserts.
- Outputs for the signal pass: `topology` (the program's), `paths` (`ExpandJunctionLanes`, checked against the program's paths: in port p is the p-th W slot, out port p the p-th E slot from the last), `graph` (one node per program node: split slot = the in slot of its In edge, merge slot = the out slot its tree feeds, level 0, Plain; one edge per program edge in order, kind In from a port to a split, Out from a merge, else Leg, `paths` ascending, width 1; `crossings` = each Bridge's (edge, crossed edge), sorted), `programs` (`edges` and `copies` sized to the edges, every list empty except a doubled edge's), `track_routes` and `prio`. The block does not call the signal pass.
- Tags: one `JunctionTrackPieceTag` per piece, token RUN, run 0, copy 0, and the edge follows the convention of `JunctionRouteEvents`. The piece at a split node's tile belongs to the edge that leaves the split (the moves already put it there). A merge's tile and every piece before it belong to the edge that ends at the merge node, and the pieces after it to the edge that leaves. So the through route's merge tile (emitted by the leaving edge's moves) is retagged to the in edge, and an S-bend's closing axis piece, one tile past the joiner's merge tile, is retagged to the leaving edge.
- A prio span is built with the placement's `JunctionPrioStepBits`: merge = (the node's column, the fixed in edge's line), side NW when the prio line is above the fixed line, else SE, steps 0 to P (even) at column merge - step, the lane tile's bits (even steps) then the row tile's.
- A doubled edge (D80) holds copy 1's segments in `JunctionBlockEdge::copy`; its `segments` are copy 0's. An S-bend segment may carry a role, LaneSplit (on copy 1) or LaneMerge (on copy 0). The generator works on an expanded program: copy 1 is an extra edge from the same node to the same end, and every path through a doubled edge has a second path with copy 1 in its place. Every rule family applies to each copy as its own track; the two copies of one edge are related for R7.
- R2 and R3 also count the other copy's bends: the tiles of the copy 1 LaneSplit bend on a copy's head line end before that copy's near head with one plain axis tile between, and the tiles of the copy 0 LaneMerge bend on a copy's head line start after that copy's far head with one plain axis tile between. R4 holds per deck, against the crossed edge's pieces on that deck's line.
- `routes` and `track_routes` hold one route per path, and for a path through a doubled edge two, copy 0 then copy 1, each port to port; `track_routes` carry `copy` 0 or 1, and `paths`, `graph` and `programs` keep the source's paths and edges.
- For a doubled edge e, `programs.edges[e]` is one SBend step with role LaneMerge and the hand of copy 0's bend, and `programs.copies[e]` one SBend step with role LaneSplit and the hand of copy 1's bend (hand -1 when the first curve is left, else +1). The half pieces of the LaneSplit bend (copy 1) and the LaneMerge bend (copy 0) are tagged token 0 with their copy; every other piece is token RUN with its route's copy. `JunctionRouteEvents` finds one lane split and one lane merge on each copy's route.
- The exit block (points W, p1, s0, s1, ls, hN0, hF0, hN1, hF1, q0, E; lines: l0 0, l1 1; out l0a 0, l1a 1, l0b 3, l1b 4): l0 is Run(s0); l1 is Run(p1), SBend(+3), Run(s1); l0a Run(E); l1b Run(E) on line 4; l1a SBend(-3) then Run(E); l0b copy 0 is SBend(+2), Run(ls), Run(hN0), Bridge(hF0) over l1a, Run(q0), SBend(+1, LaneMerge), Run(E), copy 1 SBend(+2), Run(ls), SBend(+1, LaneSplit), Run(hN1), Bridge(hF1) over l1a, Run(E). Its topology has W groups l0, l1 and E groups l1b, l0b, l1a, l0a in slot order, four movements and no prio list.
- A program declares its bundles (`in_bundles`, `out_bundles`, copied to the block): port indices per bundle, each in ascending line order; bundle 0 is on the trunk lines, bundle 1 the diverging bundle. Exit: in {{l0, l1}}, out {{l0a, l1a}, {l0b, l1b}}. Join 227: in {{fA, cA}, {cB, fB}}, out {{L0, L1}}.
- The 227 block's L1 fans in after its merge: Run(f1), SBend(-4), Run(E), so its out lanes L0 and L1 leave side by side on lines 1 and 2 (out_lines {1, 2}); the block owns this, the core's out edge is an ordinary bundle.
- `OrientJunctionBlock(block, heading, mirrored)` turns a block into a hub frame: frame (a, c) lands at a V(h) + (mirrored ? -c : c) R(h) (`OrientJunctionVector` with rotation (1 - h) mod 4, or mirrored with (3 - h) mod 4), tracks and trackdirs by the same mirror-then-rotate transforms as `ApplyJunctionOrientation`, route moves with L and R swapped when mirrored. Every offset is from the anchor, the in port of track 0 of in bundle 0 (the lowest-line port of in bundle 0, the highest when mirrored). A bundle's tracks run left to right of travel (descending frame line when mirrored). `tiles` are the layout's track tiles (bridge heads and middles carry no track), `prio_tiles` the prio spans', `length` the out-port column E.
- `JunctionOrientedBlock::prio` holds the block's prio spans turned like its tiles: merge and tiles as offsets from the anchor, bits through the same track transform, drawing order kept.
- `ComposeJunctionHub` (`junction_hub.h`, DESIGN-blocks §7.3) composes the core's placement and render with its oriented blocks into a `JunctionHub` {layout, topology, paths, graph, programs, routes, prio}:
  - Hub slot of (one-lane slot, track) is the track's rank among its group's slots by port tile: W y ascending, S x ascending, E y descending.
  - Hub lane paths are found by walking from each hub in slot through the blocks' paths: a block out port (bundle, track) gives the core edge by the node's role and its track, which meets the next block's port of the bundle its role names. `prio` holds when the last join block path on the walk is on that block topology's prio list (D301).
  - A route per hub path and per copy through a doubled block edge: each core track's render pieces, then the block's oriented route rasterised at the anchor (`placement.nodes[v]`), and so on. A core piece is tagged {hub edge, RUN, tokens of the hub edge before it, route copy}; a block piece keeps its block tag with the block edge mapped to its hub edge, its token and run offset by the tokens before its block edge in that hub edge, and the route's copy.
  - The signal graph has every block node of every instance, and one hub edge per chain: a block edge to an out port, the core track it meets and the next block's edge from the matching in port are one edge, likewise at a hub port. Kind is In from a hub port, Out to one, else Leg. `crossings` are each block's mapped plus every core crossing over every track pair, sorted without duplicates. `programs.edges` and `copies` carry the tokens of a doubled block edge only.
  - The layout is the core's with every block route's raster added; the box stays the placement's (no normalise, every tile must lie inside it). The prio spans are every block's at its anchor.
  - A core track or block edge on no hub path is a compose error.
