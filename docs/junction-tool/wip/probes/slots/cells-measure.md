# cells-2: do the convex cells decide the keep-apart sides? (measurement, no production change)
Measured in the same hidden `[.][cellsmeasure]` test (`CellsSidesAccumulate`, `PLANAR_CELLS_REPORT=<file> build-rel/openttd_test '[cellsmeasure]'`, 197 via `PLANAR_CELLS_197=<file>` with `[planardomain197]`); the report's `cells-2 <fixture>` block. Pairs = every pairwise keep-apart pair with rows (the judge's read-out, default rules), summed over every legal drawing incl. off-interface (as sides-3..5), so pair-instances; 197 is one drawing (not legal, V's own points). The cells are `RefinePlanarShapeCells` on the contracted variant (no landing_from); a pair's walls are matched to cells by (face, track, piece, heading) from `PlanarShapeFaceWalls`; the dummies' cells form a tree per face.
- Same cell: some cell holds both walls. The convex cell puts each wall in the other's inward half-plane, so two candidates: i's inward (always c0) and j's inward (the candidate with j's inward normal from j; equal to c0 when the walls are anti-parallel). Failure = a candidate's margin below 0 on the drawing; "neither" = both negative (the two-way inward choice itself fails).
- Different cells: a dummy separates them when removing it from the cell tree puts every cell of wall i on one side and every cell of wall j on the other (a landing wall lies on both sides, so a dummy it lands on separates nothing for it). With several, the one nearest wall i along the tree path from i's cells is the "first" (not literally the first on the face walk; the cell-tree order stands for it). Direction = the normal from i's side to j's side, from the dummy's walked heading in the cell on i's side (heading + 2). Margin = the pair's own rows (the from-i candidate's rows, exemptions and cut ends as written) projected on that direction minus the spacing; where the direction equals an existing candidate (same direction from the same wall, or the opposite direction from the other wall) that candidate's own margin and spacing; otherwise the larger of the two axes' spacings.
- Coverage vs sides-4 = walk_strict or facing forces the pair. Agreement: both force/decide; same cell agrees when the forced candidate is either inward one, different cells when the dummy's direction coincides with the forced candidate.

## Reading
- The cells decide every pair (100%, 99.2% on 197) against sides-4's 15-65% (X1 32%, K1 65%, 197 30%), and never miss one sides-4 forces.
- But the decided side is not sound as a single side: on legal drawings the first separating dummy's candidate has a negative margin for 20-31% of different-cell pairs (X1 418 of 1650, K1 301 of 972, 197 398 of 1707), mostly where its direction is no pair candidate (X1 297 of 514), and where both force it differs from walk_strict/facing in 30-45% of pairs (197 336 of 741); in the same cell the single i-inward side fails for 36-39% (X1 603 of 1654, K1 278 of 709), j-inward for 0-17%, and the two-way inward choice fails for 39/16/16/16 (X1/X2/L1/197 30) pairs, all staggered anti-parallel walls separated along their length, which the inward half-planes do not cover.
- So the cells do not give a sound forced side for the pairwise rows without further exemptions (a dummy normal needs the node-wedge and S-bend allowances that make it too strict in cells-1); what they give soundly is membership (same cell versus separated by a dummy), and the two-way inward choice inside a cell, which fails only on a few percent. The fallback in STATUS stands.

# cells-1: convex cells over contracted lines, judged by the suite (D385 revisited, D431, D432)
Measured in the hidden `[.][cellsmeasure]` test (`PLANAR_CELLS_REPORT=<file> build-rel/openttd_test '[cellsmeasure]'`, 6 s Release) and the 197 read (`PLANAR_CELLS_197=<file>` with `[planardomain197]`); `CellsAccumulate` in `planar_domain_encoding.cpp`. No solves, no production change.

Method. Per polyline of every fixture's enumeration (hand-named configurations and neighbourhoods, legal incl. off interface, and illegal), once per polyline: `RefinePlanarShapeCells(cv.v)` on the CONTRACTED variant (no `landing_from`); then the program with `clearance = clear_rows = true`, `pairwise = false`, `program_out`, `build_only`, the default rule set otherwise (DEFAULT_RULES: span, lead, endnode, crosspiece, grid, portzero; off-interface legal drawings without the port and box rows, as the judge does), checked with `CheckPlanarProgramOnPoints` plus the lead rows and the suite's turn and node-turn checks. Legal = the domain accepts. Too strict = a legal drawing with a broken row of the cell program (every family, once per drawing). Too loose = the domain refuses and the cell program accepts.

Dummy landing points. The checker eliminates a variable only from an equality row with one unknown or two rows on the same two unknowns; a dummy's end, length and landing parameter are four rows on four unknowns, so a dummy whose ray and landing wall are both off the axes stays undetermined. Left as is, `land` and `clear` rows touching it are skipped silently (the first run: `land` undetermined in 340 of 1035 X1 drawings). So the test solves each such dummy's own `land` equalities as a small linear system (points fixed, the program's equality rows propagated, Gauss-Jordan; rank-deficient or inconsistent systems are left alone) and writes the unique solution back as equality rows (`pinned`); no pinned row ever broke. Still undetermined: dummies landing on a box side the points do not fix (X1 27 of 5058 dummies, K1 100 of 3190, L1 18 of 2560; L1 and TS2 drawings have no E or S port, so the box size is free and every row against it is skipped). The counts of broken `clear` and `land` rows are therefore lower bounds on those fixtures.

## Reading
- Cells over contracted lines are sound as a refinement (refused only on illegal drawings, convex on every fixture drawing, 162 dummies instead of 878 on 197, 1 length-dependent landing there), but the cell ROWS are too strict on the suite's own legal drawings: `clear` breaks on 146/146, 83/83, 9/9, 112/112, 8/8, 6/6, 2/2, 1/1, 1/1 legal drawings and 99/118 on K1, `land` (a ray cannot reach its named wall) on about half of X1/K1 and most of L1/Y1.
- The breaks sit at the node wedges and S-bends the dummies cut into small triangles (first examples: a vertex one half tile from a one-unit diagonal), the same mechanism as D431's reason (3) and shape-4; on V's 197 the cell rows break 353 times (325 `clear`, 28 `land`) on a drawing whose only other refusals are the 5 rebuild artefacts.
- So convex cells give no known sides at row level without per-cell exemptions (node wedges first), and contraction fixes the dummy count and landings (D407's reason (2)) but not the row's strictness; too loose is not measurable on these enumerations (no illegal drawing is refused by keep-apart alone). The fallback in STATUS (lazy rows, reversing D381) stands.
