# Diagnosis: why the shape program's shapes fail or grow on LL² (at 21cdedd3e6, PLANAR_SHAPE=program)

Answer to the coordinator's priority question (unwrapped headings): NOT the cause. In all six variants examined (o49t0f1s0, o2t0f0s0, o2t4f0s0, o4t0f0s0, o4t3f0s0, o5t0f0s0) every channel's net turn (unwrapped sum of the 45-degree steps) equals the default's: no channel differs by a multiple of 8 or changes sign. The channels that differ are net-0 or net-equal channels whose turns sit in a different place or direction (e.g. C2b 676 -> 656, A2b 454 -> 434, B1a 656 -> 676). The face sums themselves were not computed (SpikeWalks is not in HEAD; the spike patch code needs it); the evidence is the crossing table below.
Real cause: the program fixes the crossing heading relation only as "under - over = a + 4k, a in 1..3", so the under line may cross the over line from either side (sign) and at 45 or 90 degrees (magnitude), at equal cost. Face rows would fix the sign; nothing fixes the magnitude.

Table: rel = (under heading - over heading) mod 8 at each channel crossing, default vs program (1..3 = under crosses leftwards, 5..7 = rightwards; 2/6 = 90 degrees, 1/3/5/7 = 45).
- Q1 o49t0f1s0: X4 (C2b under B1a) 1 -> 7 and X5 (A2b under F1a) 1 -> 7: SIGN flips, the line crosses the over line from the other side. Also X0, X1 6 -> 5 (magnitude only). Sides rule failures (round 0 decided by sides, round 1 infeasible): the clash that finally fails is AFj2 x F1a (side +1), next to the flipped X5 (F1a/A2b); other decided clashes sit on the flipped lines (B1a x C2, C2b x CBj1, A2b x F1). The program's D1 also drops the default's 1212... jog (01212121212 -> 012).
  o2t0f0s0 and o2t4f0s0: one sign flip, X4 (B1a under C2b) 7 -> 1; the program changed only ch19 B1a (656 -> 676). Every other crossing equals the default.
  So yes: the shape makes a line approach a crossing from the side opposite to the one the topology demands (the crossing orientation is fixed by the face map; the default keeps it, the program does not).
- Q2 o4t0f0s0 / o4t3f0s0 (base infeasible; both identical): no sign flip. X1 (D1 under F1) 6 -> 5 and X0/X2 change (X0 2 -> 2, X2 6 -> 6): the only change is X1, a 90 degree crossing made a 45 degree one. IIS core (16 rows, PLANAR_IIS output in o4t0f0s0.iis in the scratch dir): crossing rows F1"2#1>B1#5 and F1"2#1>D1#7 (deck of F1 over both B1 and D1), deck rows F1"2#1 (>= 4 head gaps), "deck max" (deck <= 24), D1"0#1 deck, the D1 segment chain D1#3..#6, a tie row, and "L685: D1#5.L + D1#6.L >= 7" (a minimum-length row on two D1 pieces). Not curve spacing or train length: it is bridge geometry. A 45 degree crossing makes the footprint of each crossing longer, so one F1 deck over B1 and D1 plus D1's own deck and minimum pieces cannot fit the 24 cap.
- Q3 o5t0f0s0 (+625; 94x63 -> 104x73) and o5t4f0s0 (+617; 94x65 -> 104x73): one change, X0 (F1 under B1; B1 over) 6 -> 5, i.e. 90 -> 45 degrees. Same turns, same signs. Lengths: F1 and F2 keep their length but end 20 further east; B1 +20, B2 +20, CD1/CD2 +20, EO1/EO2 +20, SO1/SO2 +20 (+16 in t4). The shallower crossing needs the F lines to run 20 further across B, pushing every line that ends on that side out; box +10 wide and +10 high.
- Q4 Missing ingredients:
  (a) crossing orientation (sign): for each channel crossing, the sign of (under - over) is given by the face map / crossing order: rel in {1,2,3} or {5,6,7} fixed. Smallest addition: one binary-free row per crossing, hu - ho = s*a (+ 8k with the same k set as the heading unwrapping), s from the topology. This is a RULE (derived from the topology's crossing order). It would have avoided the o49 and o2 failures (4 + 1 flips).
  (b) crossing angle: the program treats 45 and 90 degrees as equal cost, and ties go to the solver. All o4 failures and all o5 growth come from a 90 -> 45 degree choice at one crossing (X1 / X0). Smallest addition: prefer a = 2 (90 degrees) at every crossing, as a small cost on |a - 2| per crossing, or as a rule that the over line's heading and the under line's differ by 2 mod 4 when both are free. Cost is an OPINION (all default crossings in LL² are 90 degrees except X2 3 in o49; none is 45 in o2/o4/o5), though geometry supports it (a deck over a 45 degree crossing is longer by width/sin45, and a deck holding two crossings can break the 24 cap). Harder as a rule: some hubs may need 45 degree crossings, so a cost is the safer form.
  Both additions are in terms of headings at crossings only.
