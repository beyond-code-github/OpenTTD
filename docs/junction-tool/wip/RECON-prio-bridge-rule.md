# Recon: what coop means by "not bridging the prioritised line" (read-only, 2026-11-18)
F = fact (quoted/cited), I = inference. S = OneDrive scratchpad; site = S/coop-site/site. Pages read as text; images not re-viewed (see RECON-prio-bridge.md for them).

## 1. Every corpus statement on bridges/tunnels and prios
- site/Balancer.html (= Joiner.html, Balancing.html copies), h2 "Bridges (also tunnels) and evil mode": "evil mode ... trains endlessly slow each other down in a cycle on doubled bridges ... it needs a slowdown - and slowdowns can happen in merges when trains join lines."
- Same, h3 "Self-unblockable bridges": "the eventual priority has to be adapted to that as you cannot use any entry check on the bridges (cant use PBS, nor entry signal to stop trains when both bridges are full)."
- Same, h3 "Full priority": "give priority to the bridges and hope that it does not do evil. ... too long priority which causes less trains to join the line - while it still can cause the bridges to evilmode and jam."
- Same, h3 "Prio to unbridged lines": "If we choose from lines which do not have bridges, we automatically cannot get evil mode on those lines."
- Same, h3 "No doubled bridges": "If we have a split to two bridges, we can make each of the bridges lead to a different line."
- Same, image captions (images unavailable in the archive): "Pre-signals on slowed bridges keep evil mode." "PBS on slowed bridges keep evil mode." "Basic signals on slowed bridges can deal with evil mode." "If the bridges can deal with it, we can use shorter priority." "When bridges are fragile, they need long priority." "Not bridging the prioritized line overcomes this problem entirely." "Making no double bridges requires adapting the hub in general. From psg 255".
- site/Evil_mode.html (Destructive Interference): "trains exiting a double-bridge or tunnel interfere ... Also referred to as Slow mode or Evil mode"; chain of events is at "the merging point ... the track leaving the merge is temporarily blocked"; contributing factors: "Proximity to merges (Particularly priority-less merges or those prioritized against the bridged track)", "Long bridges - ... 1.5x the train length or longer", "High train holding capacity within the doubled system". Fix: "One track off the bridge has been given priority; A signal has been removed so that the priority has sufficient look-ahead"; "the look-ahead cannot exceed the length of the longest bridge in the system"; "ideal prio length is two tiles, that is, an exit signal, one empty track tile, normal signal."
- site/Priorities.html (= Prio.html, Prios.html) h2 4 "Standard prio over a bridge": "The risk ... is that the gap between the connections to the parallel track becomes longer than the train on the mainline, in which case the prio won't trigger. ... un-usable for networks with trainlengths shorter than 4." h2 5: "Presignal prio over a bridge ... doesn't need third bridge to function and is also friendly to low TLs." h2 8 "PBS prio over a bridge": no parallel track; "use twoway signals at bridge exit". h2 9: "Priority built over used tunnel using PBS and combo pre-signal".
- site/Basic_Junctions.html, Basic_Networking.html: bridges/tunnels on mainlines are doubled by load; sideline bridges need not be doubled (not prio rules).
- junctionary-mirror/pages: no page text found pairing bridge with prio beyond the above (grep of bridge+prio: only the mirrored wiki pages; Priority Merge.mediawiki has no bridge/tunnel word). Corpus images: none re-read.
- RULES-coop.md R31 (l.58), R39, R40 (l.72), R50, R51 (l.93) restate these; sources are exactly the quotes above (MT = Balancer text, ABR06 for R40).

## 2. Track covered (per segment)
- The rule is stated for "the prioritized line" only. The wiki never says "coverage", "signal block" or "within N tiles".
- Main line inside coverage: NOT stated as a no-bridge zone; the same page lists prio over a bridge as a standard coop pattern (Priorities h2 4, 5, 8). The wiki bans a prio that has an entry check ON the bridges (F, "cannot use any entry check on the bridges"). I: "bridge doesn't sit within coverage" is stricter than any corpus rule.
- Main line upstream where a held train stands: not covered by any statement.
- The prioritised line as a whole (a doubled bridge anywhere on the input line that wins the merge): stated by name only ("the prioritized line"). The evil-mode mechanism (Evil_mode.html) needs the doubled bridge's exits to meet at a merge, so I: the relevant bridges are doubled bridges whose two exits lead into the prio'd merge (or a split/merge pair shortly before it), not any bridge far upstream. "Proximity to merges" is a stated factor with no distance given.
- Prio row: not covered (no statement). The joiner (non-prio line): "Prio to unbridged lines" speaks of "lines which do not have bridges" we choose FROM, i.e. the lines that feed a merge; read with Evil_mode ("prioritized against the bridged track" is a risk factor) the unprioritised, bridged line is the risky-for-others case. I: a bridged joiner is not forbidden.
- The merge's out line: not covered. Evil mode needs the out line "temporarily blocked" (Evil_mode.html), a cause, not a placement rule.

## 3. Mechanism (as stated)
- Doubled bridges hold trains on two unsignalled-spans; once something slows exit, two tracks "each release a train in turn from a stand still" and keep blocking each other at the merge (Evil_mode.html). A prio on the bridged line makes it keep going at full speed and empties it ("allowing one of the tracks to empty at full speed", Evil_mode.html). The opposite reading in the Balancer page: with the prio on the unbridged line, the bridged line (loser) just queues; "we automatically cannot get evil mode on those lines" refers to the lines prioritised, so the doubled-bridge line must be the unprioritised one, or the prio is not on the bridged line. F-ambiguous: Balancer's three adjacent captions (long prio for fragile bridges; not bridging the prioritised line) are images, unreadable, so exactly which of the two lines carries the bridge is not settled. I: "prioritised line" = the line whose trains get the green (the ML at our merge).
- The "cannot use an entry check on the bridges" is a separate mechanism: signals sit only on plain tiles (rail_cmd.cpp:1077, per RESEARCH-prio-freedom.md:7), so a bridge span is one block member.

## 4. Tunnel versus bridge; row or joiner on a bridge
- Tunnel = bridge in the rule: the heading and Evil_mode both say "bridges (also tunnels)" / "double-bridge or tunnel". F.
- Prio row on a bridge: "Standard prio over a bridge" needs a "third bridge" (the row bridged, F Priorities h2 4); "Presignal prio over a bridge" does not (h2 5). Both are offered without evil-mode warning, and F: they concern a single bridge on the line, not a doubled pair. A doubled ML bridge inside a prio's coverage is not shown in any text (RECON-prio-bridge.md s1, same finding).
- Joiner on a bridge: not covered.

## 5. Rule for the toggle-off case (our terms)
For the prio of a merge, bridge-the-prio-line off means: the main-line lane that wins that merge (the prioritised line, F "Not bridging the prioritized line") carries no bridge or tunnel on any of its pieces from the merge back to the unit that is the join of the farthest prio segment plus one segment (I: the held-train tile, D108); in particular no doubled bridge whose two exits feed that lane before the merge (F: evil mode lives on doubled bridges feeding a merge). Bridges on other lines (joiner, a further-upstream ML before a split) are allowed (I: not stated). Bridges or tunnels may stay elsewhere on the same ML upstream beyond that stretch (I: "Proximity to merges", Evil_mode.html, gives no distance; the only length cited is the bridge's own, "look-ahead cannot exceed the length of the longest bridge in the system"). Counted in coverage units and signal blocks: a bridge is one block member (F, signal.cpp:382-395 via RESEARCH-prio-freedom.md), so forbid a bridge in any block that lies between the merge signal and the held-train tile; a bridge whose entire block sits beyond that is acceptable in our model (I).
- Parts marked F: "prioritised line is not bridged"; no entry check on a bridge; tunnel = bridge; doubled-bridge exits meeting at a merge cause evil mode. Parts I: the exact stretch (coverage plus one segment), the allowance for non-prioritised and further-upstream bridges.
- The corpus does NOT settle: (a) how far upstream of the merge a doubled bridge may sit; (b) whether the rule covers a single (undoubled) bridge, since the prio-over-a-bridge patterns are given as normal coop practice (Priorities h2 4, 5); (c) which of the two lines the images of "Not bridging the prioritized line" show. Engineer should read the Balancer page images in the live wiki / psg 255 if (b) or (c) matters; the cheapest safe toggle-off is the strict one above (nothing bridged from the merge to the held-train tile, doubled or not).
