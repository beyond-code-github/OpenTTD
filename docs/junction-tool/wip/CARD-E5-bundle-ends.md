# CARD: E5 crowding at bundle ends (T3, LL2)

Status: for the engineer. Written 2026-11-22 after the side probes (ng1 7372d911eb..d295346141), sibling lanes (098679a270) and the failed common-run extension (patch wip/probes/e5land/sibext-failed-098679a270.patch).

## Context
- The keep-apart conflicts left on T3 (slack 13) and LL2 (10-12) are between lanes of one bundle where the bundle opens or closes. T3: D3's last pieces (13-17 of 18) against D2 as D3 heads for its join with E3. LL2: B1#2-4 against B2 near the AB fray.
- Sibling lanes as offset curves between their first and last channel crossing (D388 spine) are built behind `sibling_lanes`. They cover mid-trunk only and change nothing (T3 13, LL2 12).
- Extending the offset range outward while the pieces match makes T3 infeasible. Near the joins, E3's prio row and E3's copy lie between D1 and D2, so "one lane apart" is false there. The real offset is one lane per track between them, and it changes at the nodes where those beside tracks open and close.
- DESIGN-sss-v2 §2 already names this: a corridor is "a face inside a bundle (between two lanes, a lane and its row, a separator and its copy, or inside a crossing lattice). Rigid by rule: its width is exact." Today the drawing program makes rigid only a beside track against its parent chain (corridor family, D421) and sibling lanes between crossings. Every other inner-bundle face gets pairwise keep-apart with sides read from drawings (D418), and that is where the slack sits.

## Options
1. **Corridor faces rigid (D388 as designed), built generally.**
   - Per stretch, the tracks of a bundle (lanes, rows, copies) have a lateral order read from the track map's faces; each adjacent pair is offset by exactly one lane (the corridor row form).
   - A track that enters or leaves the order at a node does so by an exact one-lane opening or closing step, as the corridor family's beside tracks do now.
   - Pairwise keep-apart (D418) remains only between bundles (free faces).
   - Consequence: the conflicts left are inside corridors, so this removes them by construction or names the node rule that cannot hold (an upstream gap).
   - Cost: a real design pass, which I would do. It needs (a) which faces are corridor faces, read from the map, (b) the lateral order and its changes at nodes, (c) how the node rules (D398-D401) meet the opening and closing steps. Then one probe on the five hubs behind an option.
2. **Node-zone rule only.** Leave mid-trunk as now and add a rule for the stretch between a bundle's last crossing and its node (lanes peel off in lane order, each at least one lane from the next).
   - Consequence: smaller, but it is a special rule for one zone, where option 1 is the general one.
3. **Park the T3/LL2 crowding.**
   - T1, T2 and 197 are legal. Move to the roadmap items (corridor family on by default in `CheckPlanarDrawable`, coverage, node legs), and come back when Slots is built on the same rows.
   - Consequence: E5's verdict on T3 and LL2 stays "not legal" for now.

## Recommendation
Option 1. It is the plan of record (D388, DESIGN-sss-v2 §2 and §3.3) rather than a new rule, every probe so far points into corridor faces, and Slots needs the same rows later. Time-box: one design note plus one probe. If the probe still leaves slack on T3 or LL2 inside a corridor face, take option 3 and record the gap.

## Gemini on turn-regularity (2026-11-22, prompt wip/gemini/2026-11-22-turn-regular.md), digested
- References, which it says it is sure of: Bridgeman, Di Battista, Didimo, Liotta, Tamassia and Vismara, "Turn-regularity and optimal area drawings of orthogonal representations", CGTA 16(1), 2000 (no kitty corners, so the visibility orders are fixed and compaction needs no disjunctions); Patrignani, "On the complexity of orthogonal compaction", CGTA 19(1), 2001 (NP-hard in general). The cure is dummy edges or ordering constraints between kitty corners. To be checked against the papers before the PAPER notes cite them.
- **The key caveat:** no octilinear analogue is known. With 45-degree walls, even a convex face can leave the separating axis of a wall pair dependent on lengths (east wall against north-west wall across north and north-east walls). So resolving kitty corners removes the hooking cases, but it does not make every side follow from headings. Some disjunction is intrinsic to octilinear keep-apart.
- How to resolve a kitty corner: an ordering row between the two reflex corners along one of the four axes (x, y, x+y, x-y), not a dummy edge with a landing (landings depend on lengths). It is linear, with no binaries once the order is chosen.
- LL2 rule: do not open or close a copy (or prio row) inside a face bounded by an opposing-turn kitty corner unless a lane of slack is allocated; end the copy before the bend zone or carry it past.
- Trap: enumerating kitty-corner orders as variants is exponential (197 might have 20-40 pairs). Only tight pinches count (same or adjacent bundle, opposing bends at one station, a few hops apart), with the default order taken from travel (the outer or upstream lane bends first) and a variant bit only for head-on bends of independent bundles.
- **My reading:** by construction in the strict sense (every side from headings, no choices) is not available in octilinear geometry. The architectural question becomes where the remaining separation choices live and who owns them: (a) Shape decides kitty-corner orders by rule, and Slots keeps bounded drawing-read sides (D418) for the length-dependent rest; (b) Slots owns the choices exactly, with local per-face binaries (fast with a tight bound: 528 binaries in 3 s on T3), which amends DESIGN-sss-v2 §3.3 "no binaries for keep-apart"; (c) both. Falsifier for (a) before building: read the exact T3 certificate drawing's sides at its kitty-corner pairs and check whether the travel-order rule predicts them.

## Where this leaves the architecture (summary for the engineer, 2026-11-22)
Proven:
1. **LL2** is undrawable with its current Structure and Shape. The minimal core is F1's copy rejoining F1 inside the face where D1's and B1's bends face each other: an upstream gap. Not the prio rows: removing them leaves identical headings and the same slack.
2. **T3** is drawable face by face; its slack is wrong side picks (39 of 132 in the crowded faces contradicted by the exact drawing).
3. **Side choices are length-dependent.** Facing pairs are decidable from headings (D426 probe, valid). Inward-only is refuted by V's 197. Gemini: no octilinear turn-regularity theorem.
4. **Exact choices scale only locally:** faces in seconds, whole hubs not in 15 s.

So by construction in the strict sense (every side from headings, no choices) is not available. The choices have to live somewhere. The options, all yours to rule on:
- **(A) Shape decides the choices that matter upstream.** It orders the across-face pairs (kitty corners and kin) by rule from topology (travel order in a bundle, as Gemini suggests), plus a promise clause that rules out LL2's configuration. Slots stays an LP with no binaries. Risk: the rule must match length-dependent reality, and inward-only, the simplest such rule, failed on V. A topological rule needs designing and checking against V before any code.
- **(B) Slots owns the choices with exact local solves:** a face-by-face exact model as Slots' designed method, with the faces coupled through the shared tracks. Exact per face, but solving the faces in sequence is a decomposition, so global optimality and even feasibility are not guaranteed. That makes it close to the repair you rejected, unless it is formulated as a proper decomposition (e.g. Benders) with a proof.
- **(C) Keep D418 (sides from drawings) and accept heuristics in the oracle.** E5 is a test oracle, not the production layer. Mark T3 "drawable, pipeline misses it" (the face certificates are the evidence) and move on. Slots, the real layer, faces the same question later.
- **LL2, whatever is chosen:** Structure or Shape must not close a copy inside a face holding facing bends (Gemini's rule). That is a promise clause with its own card.

My recommendation: (C) for E5 now, since the oracle's job of naming upstream gaps is done for T3/LL2 (LL2 named one, T3 has none), and the LL2 promise clause as the actionable upstream fix. Then design the separation question properly for Slots, starting from (A) with V's 197 as the answer key for any rule, before building. Reversible: yes.

## Why 10 of V's tracks do not pin (2026-11-22, script over psg197 steps.txt / lengths.txt / shape.txt and the vres log)

- **F, flex placement exclusion (L89):** V steps between two crossings on one piece (`at L89: L78@0, L74@0`, crossing-crossing); flex is not placed inside a crossing lattice. One stretch.
- **O, step order (L8):** V steps right then left on one piece (`0 1 0 -1 0`, lengths 14 2 14 4 12); flex C always puts the left pair before the right, so V's lengths on our headings land x 4 half tiles off the pin (167 vs 163: ours -2+4, V's +2-4). A representation limit, like R.
