# For Gemini: step back. Is our real problem rules the drawing doesn't codify?

## Where we are, honestly
- The drawing program (E5) is an LP/MIP oracle: given Structure's track map and Shape's headings, can lengths be found that obey every rule (pieces, ports, nodes, crossings, decks, bays, curve spacing, node gaps, corridors, keep-apart)?
- **Legal now:** T1, T2 and PSG 197 (an expert hub, V's build, slack 0, 107x151 against V's 101x124). 197 got there through an **answer key**: we pushed V's real geometry through our rows, saw which rows it broke, and fixed the over-strict rules (two keep-apart skip gaps, port gaps, deck gaps, crossings at bends).
- **Not legal:** T3 (slack 13) and LL2 (10), all of it keep-apart rows. These are our own topologies with no expert build to compare against.
- Today I ran a chain of hypotheses on T3/LL2, each refuted in turn:
  1. Guessed keep-apart sides from a bad seed. Per pair, every pair has a side that fits alone. Jointly (binaries on just the stretched pairs): T3 12, LL2 7.
  2. Sibling lanes not tied as a bundle. I built exact offset-curve rows for lanes of one channel between channel crossings (D388's spine): no gain.
  3. Corridor faces with guessed sides. I fixed the side of every "facing" wall pair from Shape's headings (the face walk monotone both ways): those pairs all hold, and the slack doesn't move.
  - What is left is corner pairs at bends: lanes of one bundle where one bends towards its join, and in LL2 an eastbound and a westbound track bending away from each other half a tile apart.
- Every read-out had heuristic sides inside it, so none of them proves anything. The engineer rightly asks how we can be sure of anything.

## What a holistic look suggests (our hypothesis, not yet tested)
- The hubs that fail are exactly the ones with **multi-lane prio joins and frays**. T3 and LL2 have prio rows (a track beside a lane over its join, for the prio signals) and copies (a full-share lane doubled over a crossing). PSG 197 has neither prio rows nor copies. T2 has them but is small.
- Our layers have no layout rule for a multi-lane prio join. We deferred a component catalogue (D401: "if we don't need any real gadgets yet for 197, defer") on 197's evidence, and 197 has no prio joins.
- **LL2 is "the PSG 227 pattern at two lanes: his graph, our prio element."** The prio element (rows, plus copies over crossings) is ours, not the expert's. In the real PSG 227 hub, every join is a three-input join (fixed prio lane plus two choice lanes meeting on one tile or adjacent tiles), gated by entry pre-signals, with path signals and "prio over a doubled bridge". So for LL2 there may be no expert layout of our prio element at all.
- So the main problem may be **missing component rules**: how a staggered multi-lane prio join lays out its lanes, rows and copies (order, spacing, where each opens and closes). Shape picks join headings generically, and the drawing then cannot fit them. Or our prio element itself may be harder to lay out than what experts build.

## Tests we are considering (each with what would falsify it)
- **(A) Remove the prio element:** run T3 and LL2 with no prio statement (no prio rows). If both become legal, the crowding comes from the prio element; if they stay at the same slack, it doesn't.
- **(B) Answer key on a real prio join:** extract a PSG hub that has multi-lane prio joins (PSG 227, or another) through our corpus extractor and push its geometry through our rows. The rows it breaks are over-strict rules; if it breaks none, our rules are fine and the problem is our shapes or our search.
- **(C) Exact certificate:** solve T3's affected faces with every keep-apart pair binary, tight big-M from the box, one long offline run (or CP-SAT). A drawing proves the pipeline's heuristics are at fault; infeasibility proves Shape's headings leave no room. Earlier exact attempts timed out (197 whole: 45k binaries, nothing in 60 s; today 340 binaries, no incumbent in 15 s with a loose big-M), though single 197 faces with a tight box did solve in 1.5-6 s.
