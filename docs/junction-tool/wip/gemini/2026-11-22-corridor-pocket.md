# For Gemini: keeping tracks apart inside a bundle when its lanes fan out (OpenTTD junction generator)

## Setting
- We draw a rail junction on a grid in 8 directions (N, NE, E, ... 45 degrees apart). Units are half tiles. Straight axis track runs on tile middles; diagonal pieces run between tile-edge midpoints.
- An earlier layer fixes every track's sequence of headings. The drawing step only chooses lengths (>= 0 integers) in one linear program, so all keep-apart conditions must be linear rows, written before the solve. We want no binaries and no generate-and-check loop.
- A "flex pair" on a straight stretch at heading h is the pieces h, h-1, h, h+1, h. The inserts (h-1 and h+1) may have length 0 (no step) or more, which gives a sideways step (an S-bend).
- Clearance rule (the tile rule, as a spacing): a point of one track keeps, from the line of a piece of another track at heading g, a distance along g's normal of 2 (diagonal g, or an axis g parallel to the point's own piece) or 1 (an axis g with the point's piece diagonal or perpendicular). The distance is measured as Dot(point - line point, normal), with the normal (1,0)/(0,1) for axis and (1,1)-type for diagonal (unnormalised).

## What we have (works, with a proof)
- Each face of the planar map is split into "sides". A side is a run of walls whose headings are equal once flex inserts are removed, so a, insert, b, insert, c is one side with lines through a, b and c. The face's sides turn one way (convex once the inserts are removed).
- Rows: every vertex of a side keeps the spacing from every line of every other side, except the two sides meeting at that vertex. No row targets an insert's line.
- Proof idea: every point of side S lies in its "strip" between its innermost and outermost lines. Every other side's vertices stay the spacing inside S's innermost line. A lemma about tile separation across a grid line (including the tile rule's "opposite corners" allowance on diagonals) turns that into "no shared tile".
- Known cost: an outward step's "pocket" (the space beside b between a's line and b's line) is closed to every other side.

## Where it fails (a real hub, PSG 197 built by an expert)
- Two lanes of one out port, EO1 and EO2, run parallel (heading E) to the box edge, 2 half tiles apart (exactly one lane spacing) at the port. Between them, at the far end, a third lane closes the face: it comes down diagonally (SE), crosses EO1's lane (or merges into one of them) and continues as EO2. So the face is a thin strip: top side heading W (walked), a short SE side, bottom side heading E, and the box edge.
- The SE side has vertices strictly between the two lanes (its own flex vertices, the merge node, piece joints). Each must keep 2 from the top side's lines and 1 from the bottom side's. That needs a strip about 3 wide, but the strip is 2 wide at the port. The expert's layout makes room by stepping the two lanes apart a short way from the port (a fan): the top lane steps outward. Our rule then refuses it, because the SE side's vertices must still keep 2 inside the top side's innermost line, which is the port piece's line, so the pocket the fan creates can't be used.
- 9 of the hub's 162 faces fail this way. All are bundle-interior: between lanes of one port or channel, closed by a lane crossing or merging between them.

## Questions
1. Is there a linear (no binaries), sound way to let another side use an outward step's pocket? Our idea is per vertex: "keep from a's line" only when the vertex lies before the insert along h, else from b's line only. But "before or after the insert" depends on lengths, which looks like a disjunction. For this case the order along h might be known from the topology (the closing lane lies beyond the fan, farther from the port). Can a known order be turned into linear rows, for example "the vertex's projection on h is beyond the insert's far end" as a row, plus "keep from b's line" only? Is that sound, and what else must hold (end caps at the insert)?
2. Alternatively, should bundle-interior faces not be handled as faces at all, but as "corridors": the lanes as offset curves of one spine (copies 1 lane apart, with fans and closes as synchronized steps), and a crossing or merging lane as a gadget placed in the corridor? What would the minimal linear model of such a corridor be, so that a fan's room is available to a lane crossing between the fanned lanes?
3. A gap we know about: rows keep a cell's vertices on their side of each dummy edge (a non-track edge splitting a non-convex face into convex cells), but there is no spacing across a dummy, so two cells' walls on either side of it could come closer than the spacing. Our candidate: by the same tile lemma, a dummy line on the grid separates with the spacing on one side only (rows of at least the spacing on one side, at least 0 on the other). Is that sound, and how should we choose the side?
4. Anything in the setting above that looks unsound to you, especially near nodes (two lanes sharing the node tile, which the tile rule allows) and crossings (the crossing point is shared).
