| D18 | What CL means | Curve Length: the minimum straight track (orthogonal or diagonal) between two consecutive 45-degree turns in the same direction; CL3 = 3 tiles. Matches the game: `Train::GetCurveSpeedLimit` (`train_cmd.cpp:321`) penalises short distances between same-direction 45° bends within a train; an S-bend (one left, one right) has no penalty; a 90° bend caps speed at 61. How diagonal pieces are counted is still to be confirmed. | Generators, later the solver |
| D19 | What "doubled bridges" means | "Split, 2 bridges, merge": each track that crosses the mainline splits into two parallel tracks, crosses on two side-by-side bridges, then merges back. Reason: vanilla can't signal bridges (`CmdBuildSingleSignal` needs a plain rail tile, `rail_cmd.cpp:1078`), so each bridge is one long block; two bridges let trains alternate. | Generators |
| D20 | Signal type | "I think people usually use one-way block signals, but consider the CPU implications as discussed." Default one-way block signals; signal type is a generator parameter. The junction is fully grade-separated, so path signals add no capacity there. | Generators |
| D22 | Movements and line spacing | "The mainline goes straight through, and the branch can be joined/left from either direction" (full T: all six movements). "We also need to treat the number of spaces between rail directions as configurable": the gap in tiles between the two directions of a line is a generator parameter. | Generators |
| D24 | Order of splits and merges | "Splits must happen before merges": along every track, a train meets any split before any merge. A hard rule for every generator (and later the solver). | All generators |
| D25 | Priorities (prios) | "We need to also consider prios. Prios are signal loopbacks designed to only allow trains to merge when there's space on the mainline." Merges onto the mainline get a prio: a parallel unused track joined to the mainline track at intervals upstream of the merge, so a mainline train occupying it holds the merging signal red (wiki: Junctionary/Priority Merge, "Priority Merges Using Extra Track"; works with block signals). Segmented so the shortest train always covers a connection. Details (which merges, side, length) to be settled. | Generators |
| D26 | Who designs the layout | "To be honest, we should be letting the math design the junction." The generator derives the layout from the topology, the rules and the parameters (e.g. it decides which connection bridges over which), rather than a hand-drawn layout. Approach to be agreed. | Generators |

| D67 | CL is derived, not set (revises D64's CL setting, D18's CL parameter) | "CL should be derived from the template train properties instead of being specified directly." From `Train::GetCurveSpeedLimit` (`train_cmd.cpp`, realistic acceleration): a penalty only applies when one train covers two or more same-direction 45° bends at once; the limit is `232 - (13 - s)²` for average bend spacing `s` in half-tiles (straight tile 2, diagonal piece 1; clamped 1..12), plus half per point of rail-type `curve_speed`, plus 20% if tilting; bends ≤ 1 wagon apart cap at 88; one left + one right is exempt only when they are the only bends under the train (engineer: "S-bends only count as free curves if there is one of them per TL"; `curvecount[0] == 1 && curvecount[1] == 1`). Derived CL = the smallest spacing whose limit ≥ the train's max speed, capped at TL (bends further apart than the train never penalise). 1b inputs: TL from the window's stand-in control, max speed from D45's fastest buildable compatible engine, the rail type's `curve_speed`, no tilt. 1c swaps in the picked train. Stock builds keep unknown `openttd.cfg` keys (`SaveToConfig` only removes known moved settings, `settings.cpp:1505-1525`), so switching builds is safe too. | D71 | Waiting room and deadlocks | "This is somewhat dependent on traffic - The key aspect is that traffic joining a SL should never block the ML. | D82 | Signals at every bridge | Engineer: "there's no signal before the bridge on the ML". | D83 | Whole-train waiting bays | Engineer: "If the waiting bays aren't whole trains then this will block." Measured on the reference: the N→W queue is 18 units (2.25 trains) and N→E is 21 units (2.6 trains), with the 2nd N→E train held on its bridge. Rule for every queue that ends at a merge signal (SL joiners and the outbound merge): signals at exactly k×TL units back from the merge signal (k = 1..n) along the path, so stopped trains fill whole bays. No bay boundary may fall on a switch, so no held train blocks a split. A held train may stand on a bridge (engineer, 2026-11-08: "There's no concrete rule that says trains cant leave their tail on a bridge as long as the waiting bay is utilised correctly and not blocking a split"); a boundary signal simply can't sit on a bridge tile. Capacity counts whole bays. At least one bay is required before the upstream split; more bays are traffic-dependent (D71, 1c). Balance (engineer: "You have to balance the signals with the waiting bays - we still need two trains following each other closely to be able to merge smoothly onto an empty ML"): the queue keeps full SD signalling all the way to the merge signal, so following trains run at close headway when the ML is clear. The SD grid on a queue is anchored at the merge signal and laid backwards, so the bay positions (k×TL) fall on grid signals whenever TL is a multiple of SD. Otherwise extra bay signals are added at k×TL. Bays add signals; they never replace SD spacing. | 1b checker (B5), generator |
| D84 | Traffic level as presets (settles D39's form) | Engineer: "will be good to try to pin down some throughput brackets and allow the user to select low, med, high for example". The traffic level is a Low/Medium/High choice in the Junction window (per placement, D64). Each preset maps to a throughput bracket (trains per game-hour per movement, equivalently a required headway), which drives the bridge arrangement (D32/D80), the bay counts (PLAN-1c item 7) and the prio sizing.
