## 1. Taxonomy

| ID | Rule | Why | Source | Generator impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | SLH (sideline hub): the place where a sideline meets the mainline. The sideline gives priority to the ML and must be able to join any ML rail. The easiest hub to build. | Keeps the ML fast. | JN-SLH: "we expect the side line giving priority to the main line and the side line has to be able to join any of the ML rails". "A SLH is the easiest of our hubs to build." GL: "A hub that connects a sideline to the mainline." | **design** (current generator scope; see D27, R61) |
| H2 | SLH shape depends on ML shape: straight, diagonal, corner ML, and the gap between ML lines all matter. | The geometry changes what fits. | JN-SLH: "construction depends a lot on the shape of the main line - be it a straight ML, diagonal ML, corner ML or even spaces between ML lines matters". IMG: `Psg186_SLH06.png` (Corner SLH: four connections, west split, eastbound join, east split, westbound join), `Psg191_SLH06.png` (Diagonal SLH, "ridiculously small") | **param** (ML bend: straight/diagonal/corner; gap) |
| H3 | BBH (backbone hub): "the largest type of Hub". Usually at least 3 cardinal directions of ML track, usually balanced. Connects ML segments to each other, most often in 4-way or 3-way patterns. | Network junction, not a branch. | BBH: "usually consists of at least 3 cardinal directions of mainline Track, and is also usually balanced". GL: "A hub that connects multiple mainlines with each other." | **gap** (new hub kind; generator has none) |
| H4 | MSH (main station hub): "A hub that connects main stations to mainlines." The station side is a main station (terminus, sometimes called Main station in GL), not an industry sideline. | Big stations need an ML-grade feed, not a one-lane sideline. | GL: "Main station hub: A hub that connects main stations to mainlines". GL "Terminus station / Main station: ... enter and exit from the same direction". Engineer (user brief): "a specialist type of backbone hub". | **gap** |
| H5 | Sideline vs main station feed: a sideline "connects industry stations with the mainline. It leads from stations to the sideline hub". Main stations hang off MSHs, never off an SLH or the ML directly. | Different traffic classes. | GL (Sideline). RS: "Never ever connect a station directly to the mainline." RULES-stations S10. | **check** |
| H6 | MSH is treated like a BBH in the sources: ABR06 says full-priority merges are "typical for SLHs ... Although some people use these things also on BBHs and MSHs" and lists "BBH 02" and "MSH 03" under the same composition techniques (tunnels/bridges). | MSHs are built with BBH techniques. | ABR06 (Fully Prioritized Merge; Multi-Levels; Clash Together; Joining bridges together) | **design** (implied: MSH = BBH-family) |
| H7 | 4-way hub: the old style. Every line pairs with every other. "We do not use this anymore." Each builder did it differently ("roundabouts, whirpools, just mess"). A 4-way gets "too large today". Coop moved to 3-way "ultimate 3way" plus careful components. | Size; non-unifiable design. | ABR06: "you basically can't unify it", "the 4way hubs are becoming too large today". JN: `Pile_Transport, 17 Dez 2090` entry "4-way ... Don't try this at home!" (thgergo, MainserverGame_04). IMG: `hubs_173Mark1.png` (viewed: "BBH 17 / Mark ... gaps and weirdness on purpose", four tunnel fans, lines swap sides) | **gap** (4-way not planned; see H29) |
| H8 | Invisible hub: an SLH (or sub-hub) built as split -> long tunnels/bridges -> minimal merge; the long split part is hidden under/over the other connections. | Short hub, small SLH. | ABR10: "we basically tunnel the whole hub through from one end to another". | **design** (variant of SLH; see section 2) |
| H9 | SML hub: a hub on shift mainlines. Never popular because "they are just huge"; ABR06 ignores them. Shifters sit before every SLH. | Size. | ABR06: "SML hubs are never very popular because they are just huge". SML: "Trains should have shifting opportunities prior to every SLH." | **gap** (out of scope; see D-docs on SML) |
| H10 | "Furball": a BBH with short curve length, long trains and "a flood of tunnels providing all-to-all choices". Tunnel only. | Short curves need many tunnels to give all-to-all. | JN-BBH: "Meet the Furball." (PSG 248, V453000). IMG: `Psg248_bbhfurball.png` (viewed: dense mesh of tunnel portals, no discernible T) | **gap** |
| H11 | Named BBH families by throughput of the strongest end: LL_RR, LLL_RRR, LLLL_RRRR, LLLL_RRRR+ ("massive"). Examples: 3-way with two LLLRRR and one LLRR ends (PSG197); LLLL RRRR with 5 lines in the third direction (PSG219 "Balanced"); LLLLRRRR "Snake Nest" (PSG227); "Brainmelter" (PSG186). | Line count is the main size axis. | JN-BBH tables (names as quoted) | **param** (line count per arm; arms need not match) |

## 2. SLH rules not already in RULES-coop.md

Already covered (cite, do not repeat): SLH joiner with full prio R16, R20, R61; sidelines reach ML only via an SLH R44; "split before merge" D-section. New:

| ID | Rule | Why | Source | Generator impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H12 | An SLH needs prios on the merge, and every sideline lane must reach every ML lane. Old SLHs without prios or without reach-every-lane are obsolete. | Quality bar. | JN-SLH: "In the very old times there used to be built SLHs without priorities or without ability to join every ML rail." | **check** (reach-every-ML-lane per sideline lane; with prio) |
| H13 | One line joins all others (SLH join style) works for any ML line count: "just multiply/expand the design", but retune the waiting bays when expanding. | Waiting bay length is what makes it perform. | ABR06: "for any amount of ML lines, just multiply/expand the design - also, when expanding you should toy with the waiting bays so it performs best". IMG: `hubs_1to2.png` (viewed: a single line fanning to two ML lines via a chain of diagonal crossovers, shown by two yellow arrows) | **design** |
| H14 | Fast joiner: SLH with exactly 1xTL waiting bays, so choices happen at full speed. | Waiting bay length = TL gives full-speed decisions. | JN-SLH: "SLH built with emphasis on exactly 1xTL waiting bays which allow to have full speed choices" (PSG204, V453000). | **param** (waiting bay = 1 TL; matches R20-family) |
| H15 | Invisible SLH structure: (1) split, (2) tunnels ("triple tunnels are very likely", depends on TL), (3) merge = the tunnels just end, and the tunnels carry the priority role. Surface carries left-hand traffic, two merges, and a third merge outside the hub. | Short hub. | ABR10 ("Underground part", "Surface part"). IMG: `ABR10_tunnelhub_signs.png` (viewed: labels Split, Tunnels, Tunnel End, Merger, Left-Hand Traffic, Outside of the hub) | **design** (alternative SLH style, optional) |
| H16 | Invisible hubs are train-dependent: effective with badly accelerating trains, short curves and short waiting bays; shorter trains need more tunnels (TL2 may need 4, "drastically increases the priority size"). Disadvantages: unfriendly to larger line counts, not expandable, fixed minimal prio size, BBH merger on top of tunnels is painful. | Prio must be long because the tunnels are long. | ABR10 (Curve:Acceleration Ratio; Train length effect; Disadvantages) | **param** (only for low line count; tunnel count rises as TL falls) |
| H17 | Bridges allow flexible placement, tunnels look clean. Over-long bridges: add another bridge or cut one in half with a "cut section" tunnelled by other hub parts. | Bridge length limit. | ABR10 (Other thoughts) | **design** (bridge-length cap handling) |
| H18 | Build an invisible hub from the outside (opposite the sideline side), so the left-hand traffic is inside and pieces add on from outside. Order "probably doesn't matter". | Avoids rework from an unknown bridge count. | ABR10: "build them from the outside part (the opposite of where the actual SL is heading)" | **design** (build-order hint) |
| H19 | SML: shifting happens before every SLH; if SML is used the ML lanes become imbalanced (inner busier), "to be taken into consideration during BBH and Mainline Station construction". | Merge lane is kept free. | SML | **gap** (note for any BBH/MSH balancing) |

## 3. BBH

Structure and build sequence (BBH, IMG `BBH_building_1..5.PNG`; the example is an "LL5RR to LL5RR 3-way", i.e. two LL_RR MLs meeting as a T; images 2 and 5 viewed: three arms, sign labels ML CROSSING, CROSSOVER, EXIT 1-3, JOIN 1-3 laid out on the arms):

| ID | Rule | Why | Source | Generator impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H20 | A 3-way BBH decomposes into: one ML crossing (centre), one crossover, 3 exits, 3 joins. A 4-way has more of each (implied). The exit count equals the arm count; each arm has one exit and one join (implied by "3 exits and 3 joins"). | Countable skeleton. | BBH: "one ML crossing, one crossover, 3 exits and 3 joins". Already R10 in RULES-coop. | **check** (counts; extend to N arms) |
| H21 | Build order: (1) ML crossing first, in the centre, because it is hard to add later; (2) crossover plus the first exit together; (3) the other exits; (4) joins with full balanced all-to-all non-blocking merging; (5) signals and prios last. | Building outward from the centre avoids space problems. | BBH: "It is a common mistake to start with an exit or a join; don't do this." "you can hack in priorities in any place, so you don't have to worry about reserving space for them" (R59, R30 already) | **design** (generator pass order) |
| H22 | The crossover is where the two joining ML pairs cross each other, "one or more of these in every BBH", needed to let traffic go to the other direction. | A T needs both directions of one ML to reach the third arm. | BBH: "The crossover is the point where the joining mainline cross each other". IMG: `BBH_building_2.PNG` (crossover sits between ML crossing and Exit 1/Join 2 on the SE arm) | **design** (the movement that SLH does not have; see H33) |
| H23 | Exits need no balancing. Joins (mergers) are "the hardest, most key thing mostly defining how the hub will actually look". Hub requirements: balanced (each entering lane can choose any leaving lane), efficient (no slowing), sized for the longest train. | Only merges can jam. | BBH, ABR06. R11, R58 already. | **check** |
| H24 | Merge styles (ABR06): not-balancing (luck, "relies on luck where trains come from"); fully prioritized joining all lines (SLH, 1 to N); multi-to-multi (practical only for 2-line joins); fully prioritized joining some lines (more lines join more, "doesn't balance completely"); half prioritized (two equal lines; prio that gives way "because it could make trains be stuck in them for too long"); all-to-all (systematic, fits 4->2 to X->Y, later joiners can struggle with prios, PF traps behind the merger improve balance but "are not needed"). | Which style fits depends on traffic. | ABR06 sections "Mergers"; IMG: `hubs_A2A.png` (viewed: fan of diagonal crossovers merging a left group into fewer output lines), `hubs_msh01merge.png` (viewed: a 2-line to 2-line merge with signal gaps), `hubs_PZ05.png` (not viewed) | **design** (merge style is a per-merge param; current generator has full-prio and L_R joiners only) |
| H25 | Merger signals need "sensitive gaps" for maximum throughput in multi-to-multi merges. | Throughput. | ABR06: "care about signals, making sensitive gaps is really needed". | **design** |
| H26 | Merge prios follow use: in a hub leading to a goods drop, trains from the drop give way to the ML on the A and B joins but merge equally on merge C. Different merges in one hub get different prio policy. | Think how the hub is actually used. | ABR06 "Merger Logics". IMG: `hubs_bbh04.png` (viewed; labelled BBH 04, three fans of tunnel portals on each arm; A, B, C letters are in the MSH 01 image not this one) | **design** (prio policy per merge: ML-protecting, equal, or none; D27 only covers ML-protecting) |
| H27 | Bridges vs tunnels: bridges are compact and presignal-friendly; tunnels can pass under power plants, transmitter walls. Three levels (tunnel, rail, bridge) give about 200% tracks per tile versus plain, "if you want to bother with numbers". Splits: bridging the split line or the ML are both used; "just use anything to get those lines away". | Space. | ABR06 (Bridge vs Tunnel; Multi-levels; Bridge & Tunnel abuse). | **design** (multi-level allowed; check level count) |
| H28 | Split techniques: out-of-hub (get outside, swap tracks in a safe spot; needs room, ML stays clear), solve inside (line A then B crosses it, saves space behind, costs inside), clash together (stack over/under). Direction tunnels: decide the straight line stays bridged and each hub part gets its own tunnel. BBH 02 "does not keep the lines going the same direction together". | Composition choices. | ABR06 ("Sweet-Spot"; "Directional Tunnels"). IMG: `hubs_173bbh02.png`, `hubs_173bbh14.png`, `hubs_bbh02.png` (not viewed) | **design** (hub composition style param) |
| H29 | Size: nothing numeric. The 4-way gets "too large" as lines widen because of "more complex balancers". Use "as frequent rails as possible" to fill the footprint. Real BBHs run to 4-5 lines per arm (PSG115 "5 lines", PSG219 4+4+5). | Compactness is the aim. | ABR06 (Conclusion); JN-BBH | **gap** (no formula; footprint will be what the router gives) |
| H30 | A BBH whose line counts differ per arm exists (two LLLRRR and one LLRR). Balancing is then N->M with N != M. | Real networks mix. | JN-BBH: PSG197 "Tunnel only" entry. | **param** (lines per arm independent) |

## 4. MSH

| ID | Rule | Why | Source | Generator impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H31 | MSH 03 (PSG186) is "basically mergers-only": the hub is a stack of merges with little else, using three-level bridge/tunnel stacking. IMG `hubs_186msh03.png` (viewed): a terminal-side ML feed passing through a row of four tunnel portals, with fans of crossovers on each side; `hubs_msh03.png` (viewed, "MSH 03" label): several tunnel-portal fans and a diagonal ML pair with long curves, one vertical line crossing it. | Station arrivals and departures all need to merge; splits are trivial. | ABR06: "Note how the hub is basically mergers-only." | **design** (implied: an MSH is mostly merge structures) |
| H32 | MSH 01 balancing simplification: trains from A have one track prioritized and one giving way (B). Entering the balancer from the give-way side is "uncomfortable", so trains from the give-way line (C) are shifted onto the already-joined line, then ignored in further balancing. Useful for badly accelerating trains (no stop to join). Used for an LL_RR hub, "could be used even in larger scales" (LLLL_RRRR: shift and ignore tracks, mergers get smaller). Shifters are placed inside the hub to add no space. | Fewer lanes to balance. | ABR06 "Shifters and hubs". IMG: `hubs_msh01shift.png` (viewed: three diagonal line groups labelled A, B, C, a station label "MSH 01", ML tracks entering tunnel portals) | **design / gap** (optional simplification: shift before merge) |
| H33 | MSH station side vs ML side: station arrivals "give priority on the A and B joins to the ML, while they merge equally on merge C" in the goods-drop hub: ML-to-ML traffic is prioritized, station traffic is balanced among itself. | Station traffic is not ML traffic. | ABR06 "Merger Logics" (the example hub leads "mainly to the goods drop") | **design** (implied for MSH: ML traffic has prio over station traffic, station lines merge equally) |
| H34 | MSH sizing, lane counts, station count and whether trains on station lines reverse (terminus) are not stated anywhere. Only that a main station is a terminus type (entry and exit on the same side, GL) and "usually has worse platform usage" (RULES-stations S2). | n/a | GL; ABR07 via RULES-stations | **gap** |

## 5. Differences from SLH that matter to a generator

| ID | Difference | Source | Generator impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| H35 | New movements: SLH has 2 branch movements per ML direction (branch to ML lane, ML lane to branch); a BBH T additionally needs ML-to-ML turns: both ML directions of the through ML to the third arm and back, i.e. the "crossover" plus the "ML crossing" (a left turn crosses opposing traffic: the sweet spot). 3 arms = 6 directed arm-to-arm movements. | BBH, ABR06 (Sweet-Spot) | **design** (movement set from arm graph; current T is L_R branch only) |
| H36 | Lane counts: SLH branch is usually L_R or LL_RR; BBH arms are LL_RR or wider (up to 4-5), and can differ per arm. Joining a wider arm to a narrower one needs N->M balancing (H30). | BBH, JN-BBH | **param** |
| H37 | Priorities: SLH: all merges that feed the ML protect the ML (D27). BBH: no arm is subordinate, so merges are balanced or half-prio, "full prio" optional ("not a mistake"). So prio placement needs a policy per merge rather than "ML wins". | ABR06 (Fully/Half Prioritized) | **design** |
| H38 | Balancing: SLH needs none beyond reach-every-lane; BBH merges are the balancing problem (all-to-all, "balanced" is a stated requirement). MSH may reduce it by shifting (H32). | BBH, ABR06 | **design** (a merge-style library; see H24) |
| H39 | Symmetry: BBH T from the wiki images has no obvious mirror symmetry because the third arm leaves from one side of the ML crossing. SLH T is symmetric about the branch axis only when L_R/LL_RR. (Implied from `BBH_building_2.PNG`, viewed.) | IMG | **design** (implied) |
| H40 | Sizes: SLH is small ("ridiculously small SLH", Diagonal SLH). BBH is "the largest type of hub"; 3 arms with 4-5 lines are large and need multi-level stacking. | JN-SLH, BBH, ABR06 | **param** (footprint budget) |
| H41 | Build order with exits before joins applies to BBH, and splits-before-merge is a stated safety rule already in RULES-coop. | BBH | **check** |

## 6. What the sources do not say (gaps)

- No definition of MSH beyond one glossary line; no wiki page, no Junctionary page, no rules for lane counts, how many main stations an MSH feeds, whether an MSH has both ML directions passing through, or how it differs structurally from a BBH.
- The claim "MSH is a specialist backbone hub" is from the engineer, not from a source. ABR06 treats MSH and BBH together (H6) but never contrasts them.
- No numbers: BBH footprint per line count, waiting bay and prio length for BBH merges (only the SLH 1xTL fast joiner), PF trap placement rule, shifter count.
- No explicit 4-way rules; the one 4-way example (Mark's PSG173 hub) is a showpiece. `JunctionaryBBH_4way.png` is a 75x75 icon.
- No stated rule for which ML turns a BBH must support (all six for a 3-way is the only reading; R10).
- No rule for when to choose out-of-hub, solve-inside or clash-together composition (builder judgment).
- Images only seen as screenshots; track topology could not be read reliably from any of them.
